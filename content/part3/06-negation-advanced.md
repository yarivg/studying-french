# Advanced negation and restriction

Part I covered *ne … pas* and its relatives. This chapter deals with the cases where negation
stops being straightforward: combining negatives, the *ne* that means nothing, restriction, and
what actually happens to negation in speech.

## Combining negatives

Unlike English, French stacks negative words freely — and they do **not** cancel out.

::: examples
Je ne dis jamais rien à personne. || I never say anything to anyone.
Il n'y a plus jamais personne ici. || There's never anyone here any more.
Je n'ai plus rien. || I have nothing left.
On ne voit plus personne. || You don't see anyone any more.
:::

There is only ever **one** *ne*. The order of the second elements is fixed:

> **plus** → **jamais** → **rien** / **personne**

::: warning pas cannot join the party
*Pas* is incompatible with *rien*, *personne*, *jamais*, *plus* and *aucun*. Say *je n'ai rien*,
never *je n'ai pas rien*.

The one exception is *pas encore* (not yet), which behaves as a unit: *je n'ai pas encore
mangé*.
:::

## ne … que — restriction

**ne … que** means *only*. It looks negative and is not — the sentence stays affirmative, which
has two consequences.

::: examples
Je n'ai que dix euros. || I only have ten euros.
Il ne mange que des légumes. || He only eats vegetables.
Elle ne travaille que le lundi. || She only works Mondays.
:::

::: rule Two things que does not do
**1. The article survives.** *Il ne mange que **des** légumes*, not *que de légumes*. A real
negation would collapse it.

**2. It combines with pas to mean "not only".** *Il ne mange **pas que** des légumes* — he
doesn't only eat vegetables. Here *pas* and *que* work together.
:::

**Que** attaches to whatever it precedes, so moving it changes the meaning:

::: examples
Je ne bois que du thé le matin. || Tea is the only thing I drink in the morning.
Je ne bois du thé que le matin. || The morning is the only time I drink tea.
Il n'y a que moi qui le sache. || I'm the only one who knows.
:::

The alternatives: **seulement** (a plain adverb, no *ne*) and **ne faire que** (to do nothing
but):

::: examples
J'ai seulement dix euros. || same meaning as *je n'ai que dix euros*
Il ne fait que se plaindre. || He does nothing but complain.
:::

## Le ne explétif

A **ne** that carries no negative meaning at all. It appears in formal French after certain
triggers.

| Trigger | Example |
|---|---|
| **avoir peur que**, **craindre que** | *Je crains qu'il **ne** vienne.* — I'm afraid he **will** come. |
| **avant que** | *Avant qu'il **ne** parte.* — before he leaves |
| **à moins que** | *À moins qu'il **ne** pleuve.* — unless it rains |
| **de peur que** | *De peur qu'il **ne** tombe.* |
| after a comparative | *Il est plus grand que je **ne** le pensais.* |
| **empêcher que** | *Pour empêcher qu'il **ne** parte.* |

::: warning It does not negate — read carefully
*J'ai peur qu'il **ne** vienne.* — I'm afraid he **will** come.
*J'ai peur qu'il **ne** vienne **pas**.* — I'm afraid he **won't** come.

Only the *pas* makes it negative. Misreading this reverses the meaning of the sentence.
:::

It is optional and increasingly rare. Recognise it; you never need to write it.

## sans … ni

**Sans** is already negative, so it takes no *ne* and no *pas*. Multiple items are joined by
**ni**.

::: examples
sans argent ni papiers || without money or papers
Il est parti sans rien dire. || He left without saying anything.
sans jamais se plaindre || without ever complaining
Il l'a fait sans que je le sache. || …without my knowing.
:::

::: warning After sans, use rien and personne — not quelque chose
*sans **rien** dire*, not *sans dire quelque chose*.
*sans voir **personne***, not *sans voir quelqu'un*.
:::

## ni … ni

::: examples
Je ne bois ni thé ni café. || neither tea nor coffee
Ni lui ni moi ne savons. || Neither he nor I know.
Il n'est ni grand ni petit. || He's neither tall nor short.
:::

::: rule ni … ni deletes the article
*Je ne bois **ni** thé **ni** café* — no *du*, no *le*. Indefinite and partitive articles vanish
entirely.

The **definite** article survives when the noun is specific: *Je n'aime ni **le** thé ni **le**
café.*
:::

When *ni … ni* is the subject, *ne* still precedes the verb, and the verb is usually plural:
*Ni lui ni moi **ne** savons*.

## non and pas as standalone negatives

**Non** negates a whole answer. **Pas** negates a single element, without a verb.

::: examples
Pas moi. || Not me.
Pas encore. || Not yet.
Pas du tout. || Not at all.
Pas mal. || Not bad.
Un café, pas un thé. || A coffee, not a tea.
Moi non plus. || Me neither.
Non seulement… mais aussi… || Not only… but also…
:::

**non** also prefixes adjectives and nouns: *non violent*, *un non-fumeur*, *non loin d'ici*.

## Dropping ne in speech

In conversation, **ne** disappears almost completely. This is universal, not sloppy, and it is
the norm at every social level.

| Written | Spoken |
|---|---|
| Je ne sais pas. | *J'sais pas* — often /ʃɛpa/ |
| Il n'y a personne. | *Y'a personne.* |
| Ce n'est pas grave. | *C'est pas grave.* |
| Je n'ai rien fait. | *J'ai rien fait.* |
| Il ne faut pas. | *Faut pas.* |
| Je ne veux pas. | *J'veux pas.* |

::: warning The negation still exists — it is carried by the second word
Because *pas*, *rien*, *jamais* and *personne* survive, dropping *ne* never creates ambiguity.
But if you also swallow the second word, the sentence flips meaning entirely. In fast French,
*pas* is the syllable to listen for.
:::

::: rule Understand it, but write the ne
Omitting *ne* is correct spoken usage and incorrect written usage. There is no register in
which writing *je sais pas* is acceptable outside dialogue or text messages.
:::

## Negating an infinitive

Both halves stay together, **before** the infinitive:

::: examples
Je préfère ne pas y aller. || I'd rather not go.
Il m'a dit de ne rien dire. || He told me to say nothing.
C'est important de ne jamais oublier. || …never to forget.
Ne pas fumer. || No smoking — the sign.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Combine the negatives
I never say anything to anyone. || Je ne dis jamais rien à personne.
There's never anyone here any more. || Il n'y a plus jamais personne ici.
I have nothing left. || Je n'ai plus rien.
I haven't eaten yet. || Je n'ai pas encore mangé.
:::

::: exercise ne … que
I only have ten euros. || Je n'ai que dix euros.
He only eats vegetables. || Il ne mange que des légumes. || *des* survives
He doesn't only eat vegetables. || Il ne mange pas que des légumes.
He does nothing but complain. || Il ne fait que se plaindre.
Say it with *seulement*. || Il mange seulement des légumes.
:::

::: exercise What does it mean?
*J'ai peur qu'il ne vienne.* || I'm afraid he will come. The *ne* is expletive.
*J'ai peur qu'il ne vienne pas.* || I'm afraid he won't come.
*Il est plus grand que je ne le pensais.* || He's taller than I thought. No negation.
*À moins qu'il ne pleuve.* || Unless it rains.
:::

without saying anything || sans rien dire
without money or papers || sans argent ni papiers
neither tea nor coffee || ni thé ni café
Neither he nor I know. || Ni lui ni moi ne savons.
without ever complaining || sans jamais se plaindre
:::
