# Passé simple and literary tenses

You will never say these. You will read them constantly. Every novel, most history writing,
fairy tales, newspaper obituaries and Wikipedia's historical articles use the passé simple as
their narrative past.

This chapter is for **recognition only**. The goal is that a page of Camus never stops you.

## What the passé simple does

It is the literary equivalent of the passé composé: a completed past event that moves the story
forward. In speech it died out centuries ago; in writing it survives because it keeps narrative
prose free of auxiliaries.

| Spoken / written informally | Literary |
|---|---|
| *Il **a mangé**.* | *Il **mangea**.* |
| *Elle **est partie**.* | *Elle **partit**.* |
| *Nous **avons vu**.* | *Nous **vîmes**.* |

::: examples
Il entra dans la pièce et ferma la porte. || He came into the room and shut the door.
Elle le regarda, puis sortit sans un mot. || She looked at him, then left without a word.
:::

## The endings

Three families, keyed by the vowel that runs through them: **a**, **i**, **u**.

### -er verbs: the a family

| Pronoun | *parler* |
|---|---|
| je | parl**ai** |
| tu | parl**as** |
| il / elle | parl**a** |
| nous | parl**âmes** |
| vous | parl**âtes** |
| ils / elles | parl**èrent** |

::: warning je parlai vs je parlais
*Je parl**ai*** — passé simple, /paʁle/.
*Je parl**ais*** — imperfect, /paʁlɛ/.

One letter apart. In practice, context decides: a run of narrative events is passé simple; a
description is imperfect.
:::

### -ir and -re verbs: the i family

| Pronoun | *finir* | *vendre* |
|---|---|---|
| je | fin**is** | vend**is** |
| tu | fin**is** | vend**is** |
| il / elle | fin**it** | vend**it** |
| nous | fin**îmes** | vend**îmes** |
| vous | fin**îtes** | vend**îtes** |
| ils / elles | fin**irent** | vend**irent** |

Also in this family: *dire → il dit*, *prendre → il prit*, *voir → il vit*, *écrire → il
écrivit*, *partir → il partit*, *faire → il fit*, *mettre → il mit*, *ouvrir → il ouvrit*.

::: note The singular looks like the present
*Il finit* and *il vendit* are identical to, or nearly identical to, their present forms. Only
the surrounding tenses tell you which one you are reading.
:::

### The u family

Mostly verbs whose past participle ends in **-u**.

| Pronoun | *avoir* | *être* | *vouloir* |
|---|---|---|---|
| je | e**us** | f**us** | voul**us** |
| tu | e**us** | f**us** | voul**us** |
| il / elle | e**ut** | f**ut** | voul**ut** |
| nous | e**ûmes** | f**ûmes** | voul**ûmes** |
| vous | e**ûtes** | f**ûtes** | voul**ûtes** |
| ils / elles | e**urent** | f**urent** | voul**urent** |

Also: *pouvoir → il put*, *savoir → il sut*, *devoir → il dut*, *boire → il but*, *lire → il
lut*, *vivre → il vécut*, *connaître → il connut*, *croire → il crut*, *recevoir → il reçut*.

### The genuinely irregular ones

| Infinitive | il / elle form |
|---|---|
| **venir** | il **vint** |
| **tenir** | il **tint** |
| **naître** | il **naquit** |
| **mourir** | il **mourut** |
| **faire** | il **fit** |
| **voir** | il **vit** |
| **être** | il **fut** |
| **avoir** | il **eut** |

::: tip A shortcut for recognition
For most verbs, the passé simple stem is the **past participle** minus its ending:

*vu* → *il vit*, *bu* → *il but*, *su* → *il sut*, *voulu* → *il voulut*, *pris* → *il prit*,
*mis* → *il mit*, *dit* → *il dit*.

The exceptions worth memorising are *venir → vint*, *tenir → tint*, *naître → naquit*,
*être → fut*, *faire → fit*.
:::

## Le passé antérieur

The literary counterpart of the plus-que-parfait, used after *quand*, *dès que*, *après que*.

> **avoir** or **être** in the **passé simple** + past participle

::: examples
Quand il eut fini, il partit. || When he had finished, he left.
Dès qu'elle fut arrivée, on commença. || As soon as she had arrived, they began.
:::

In modern French this becomes *quand il **a eu** fini* or simply *quand il **avait** fini*.

## L'imparfait du subjonctif

Built on the passé simple stem, with a circumflex in the third person singular. It appears where
modern French uses the present subjunctive.

| Modern | Literary |
|---|---|
| *qu'il vienne* | *qu'il **vînt*** |
| *qu'il soit* | *qu'il **fût*** |
| *qu'il ait* | *qu'il **eût*** |
| *qu'il parle* | *qu'il **parlât*** |

::: examples
Je voulais qu'il vînt. || Modern: *qu'il vienne*.
Bien qu'il fût tard, il continua. || Modern: *bien qu'il soit tard*.
:::

## Le plus-que-parfait du subjonctif

**eût** or **fût** + past participle. It has two jobs: the literary past subjunctive, and — the
part that genuinely confuses readers — a literary substitute for the **past conditional**.

::: examples
Il eût été plus simple de partir. || = *Il aurait été plus simple…* — It would have been simpler.
Si j'eusse su, je fusse venu. || = *Si j'avais su, je serais venu.*
:::

::: warning eût been can mean "would have been"
When you meet *eût* or *fût* + participle in an older text, check whether the sentence is
hypothetical. If it is, read it as a **conditional**, not a subjunctive. This form is sometimes
called the *deuxième forme du conditionnel passé*.
:::

## Reading a literary paragraph

> *Il **entra** dans la pièce. La lampe **était** allumée et le feu **brûlait** encore. Il
> **s'assit**, **ouvrit** le livre qu'il **avait apporté**, et **commença** à lire. Quand il
> **eut** terminé le premier chapitre, la nuit **était** tombée.*

The pattern is identical to modern narration, with two substitutions:

- **passé simple** (*entra, s'assit, ouvrit, commença*) does the job of the passé composé — the
  events.
- **imparfait** (*était, brûlait*) is unchanged — the description.
- **plus-que-parfait** (*avait apporté*) is unchanged — the earlier past.
- **passé antérieur** (*eut terminé*) replaces the plus-que-parfait after *quand*.

::: tip What to do when you meet an unfamiliar form
Ask three questions:
1. Does it end in **-a, -it, -ut, -irent, -urent**? → passé simple. Read it as a passé composé.
2. Does it have a **circumflex** on the third person singular (*fût, eût, vînt, parlât*)? →
   imperfect subjunctive. Read it as a present subjunctive.
3. Is it **eût / fût + participle** in a hypothetical sentence? → read it as a past conditional.

That covers essentially all of it.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Convert to modern French
Il entra dans la pièce. || Il est entré dans la pièce.
Elle partit sans un mot. || Elle est partie sans un mot.
Nous vîmes la mer. || Nous avons vu la mer.
Il fut surpris. || Il a été surpris.
Ils eurent peur. || Ils ont eu peur.
Il naquit en 1920. || Il est né en 1920.
Quand il eut fini, il partit. || Quand il a eu fini, il est parti.
:::

::: exercise Identify the tense
*il mangea* || Passé simple — a narrative event.
*il mangeait* || Imparfait — description or habit.
*il eut* || Passé simple of *avoir*.
*il eût* || Imperfect subjunctive of *avoir* — note the circumflex.
*qu'il fût* || Imperfect subjunctive of *être* — modern: *qu'il soit*.
*il avait mangé* || Plus-que-parfait — unchanged in literary French.
:::

::: exercise Which verb is it?
*il vit* || *voir* — he saw. (Or the present of *vivre*, in another context.)
*il vint* || *venir* — he came.
*il fit* || *faire* — he did.
*il fut* || *être* — he was.
*il put* || *pouvoir* — he could.
*il sut* || *savoir* — he knew, he found out.
*il naquit* || *naître* — he was born.
:::

::: exercise Read and translate
*Il ouvrit la porte, regarda dehors, et referma.* || He opened the door, looked outside, and shut it again.
*Bien qu'il fût tard, elle continua.* || Although it was late, she carried on.
*Il eût été plus simple de partir.* || It would have been simpler to leave.
:::
