# Infinitif passé and faire causatif

Two constructions that let you compress a sentence: one for saying *after having done*, one for
saying *to have something done*.

# L'infinitif passé

> **avoir** or **être** (infinitive) + past participle

Meaning: *to have done*. Its main job is to express an action that came **before** the main
verb, when both belong to the same subject.

::: examples
avoir mangé || to have eaten
être parti || to have left
s'être levé || to have got up
:::

## After après

This is where you will meet it most. **Après** is always followed by the past infinitive, never
by a present one.

::: examples
Après avoir mangé, je suis sorti. || After eating, I went out.
Après être arrivé, il s'est reposé. || After arriving, he rested.
Après s'être levée, elle a pris une douche. || After getting up, she had a shower.
:::

::: warning Never *après manger*
English says *after eating*. French cannot use a bare infinitive here — it must be **après
avoir / être** + participle.

*Après manger* is wrong. *Après **avoir** mangé* is correct.

Contrast this with **avant de**, which does take the plain infinitive: *avant **de** manger*.
:::

::: rule The subject must be the same
*Après avoir mangé, **je** suis sorti* — I ate and I went out.

If the two subjects differ, you need a full clause: *Après **que j'ai** mangé, **il** est
sorti.*
:::

## Agreement

The usual rules apply. With *être*, the participle agrees with the subject:

::: examples
Après être parti, il a téléphoné. || masculine subject
Après être partie, elle a téléphoné. || feminine
Après être partis, ils ont téléphoné. || plural
Après s'être levées, elles sont sorties. || reflexive, feminine plural
:::

## Elsewhere

The past infinitive appears after any verb or preposition that takes an infinitive, whenever
the action is earlier:

::: examples
Je regrette d'avoir dit ça. || I regret having said that.
Il est content d'être venu. || He's glad he came.
Merci d'être venu. || Thank you for coming.
Je me souviens de l'avoir vu. || I remember seeing him.
Il nie avoir volé l'argent. || He denies having stolen the money.
Sans avoir compris, il a signé. || Without having understood, he signed.
:::

::: warning Compare the timing
*Je suis content **de venir**.* — I'm glad to be coming. (Now or later.)
*Je suis content **d'être venu**.* — I'm glad I came. (Earlier.)

The past infinitive is what shifts it backwards.
:::

# Le faire causatif

> **faire** + infinitive

Meaning: to **have** or **make** something be done — by someone else.

::: examples
Je fais réparer ma voiture. || I'm having my car repaired.
Elle fait construire une maison. || She's having a house built.
Il m'a fait rire. || He made me laugh.
Le professeur fait travailler les élèves. || The teacher makes the students work.
:::

::: rule faire and the infinitive never separate
*Je **fais réparer** ma voiture.* — the object goes **after** both verbs, never between them.

*Je fais ma voiture réparer* is wrong. This is the opposite of English word order, where the
object sits in the middle: *I'm having **my car** repaired*.
:::

## Pronouns go before faire

::: examples
Je la fais réparer. || I'm having it repaired.
Il m'a fait rire. || He made me laugh.
Je les ai fait travailler. || I made them work.
Fais-le entrer. || Show him in.
:::

::: warning The participle of faire never agrees
*Je **les** ai **fait** travailler*, not *faits*. When *fait* is followed by an infinitive, it is
invariable — always.

Compare *je les ai **faits*** (I made them, no infinitive) with *je les ai **fait** travailler*.
:::

## Two objects

If both the doer and the thing done are mentioned, the doer takes **à** or **par**:

::: examples
Je fais lire le livre à mon fils. || I get my son to read the book.
J'ai fait réparer la voiture par un mécanicien. || I had the car repaired by a mechanic.
Elle a fait envoyer la lettre par sa secrétaire. || She had the letter sent by her secretary.
:::

::: note à or par?
**à** for a person you are directing — *faire lire un livre **à** un enfant*.
**par** when the emphasis is on who performed it — *faire réparer **par** un mécanicien*.

*Par* also avoids ambiguity: *j'ai fait écrire une lettre **à** Marie* could mean *I got Marie
to write* or *I had a letter written to Marie*. Using *par* removes the doubt.
:::

## se faire + infinitive

The reflexive version means something was done **to you** — often unwelcome.

::: examples
Il s'est fait voler son portefeuille. || He got his wallet stolen.
Elle s'est fait couper les cheveux. || She had her hair cut.
Je me suis fait avoir. || I got had.
Il s'est fait virer. || He got fired.
:::

Same rule: **fait** never agrees. *Elle s'est **fait** couper les cheveux.*

## laisser, voir, entendre + infinitive

These behave like *faire*, with the same word order:

::: examples
Laisse-moi passer. || Let me through.
Je l'ai laissé partir. || I let him leave.
Je l'ai vu partir. || I saw him leave.
Je les ai entendus chanter. || I heard them singing.
:::

::: warning laissé, vu and entendu can agree — fait never does
*Je les ai **vus** partir* — agrees, because *les* is the subject of *partir*.
*Je les ai **fait** partir* — never agrees.

*Faire* is the exception. Since 1990 spelling reform, *laissé* before an infinitive is also
allowed to stay invariable, so *je les ai **laissé** partir* is now equally correct.
:::

partir || être parti(e)(s)

:::

:::
