# Compound relative pronouns

Part II covered *qui*, *que*, *où* and *dont*. This chapter handles what happens when a
preposition other than *de* is involved, and when there is no noun to attach the pronoun to at
all.

## lequel and its family

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | lequel | lesquels |
| **Feminine** | laquelle | lesquelles |

They agree with the noun they replace, and they contract with **à** and **de**:

| Preposition | m. sing. | f. sing. | m. pl. | f. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (none) | lequel | laquelle | lesquels | lesquelles |
| **à** | **auquel** | à laquelle | **auxquels** | **auxquelles** |
| **de** | **duquel** | de laquelle | **desquels** | **desquelles** |

::: rule à + lequel contracts; à + laquelle does not
*auquel*, *auxquels*, *auxquelles* — but *à laquelle* stays two words. Same pattern as *au / à
la*.
:::

## After a preposition, for things

::: examples
La table **sur laquelle** j'ai posé mes clés. || the table on which…
Le projet **auquel** je travaille. || the project I'm working on
Les raisons **pour lesquelles** il est parti. || the reasons for which…
L'entreprise **dans laquelle** elle travaille. || the company she works for
Le stylo **avec lequel** j'écris. || the pen I write with
:::

::: warning English strands its prepositions; French never does
English: *the project I'm working **on***. The preposition drifts to the end.
French: *le projet **auquel** je travaille*. The preposition must come first, dragging the
pronoun with it.

There is no French equivalent of *the table I put my keys on*. The preposition and pronoun stay
welded together at the front of the clause.
:::

## After a preposition, for people: prefer qui

::: examples
La personne **avec qui** je travaille. || rather than *avec laquelle*
L'ami **à qui** j'ai parlé. || rather than *auquel*
Les gens **pour qui** je fais ça. ||
:::

Both are grammatical; **qui** is the natural choice for people. Use *lequel* for people only
when you need to be unambiguous about which of several you mean.

## duquel vs dont

Both cover *de*, so which one?

::: rule Use dont, unless the de is buried inside a longer preposition
- Plain *de* → **dont**: *le film **dont** je parle*.
- A compound preposition ending in *de* → **duquel**: *à côté **duquel***, *au-dessus
  **duquel***, *près **duquel***, *à cause **duquel***, *au bout **duquel***.

*L'immeuble **à côté duquel** j'habite.* — the building next to which I live.
*L'homme **dont** je parle.* — the man I'm talking about.

Never *à côté dont*.
:::

::: examples
La maison près de laquelle il y a un parc. || near which
Le pont au-dessous duquel passe la route. || beneath which
La réunion au cours de laquelle on a décidé. || during which
:::

## où as an alternative

For place and time, **où** is lighter than *lequel* and generally preferred:

::: examples
La ville **où** j'habite. || rather than *dans laquelle*
Le jour **où** je l'ai rencontré. || rather than *auquel*
Le pays **d'où** je viens. || from where — *d'où*, not *duquel*
Le moment **où** tout a changé. ||
:::

## ce qui, ce que, ce dont, ce à quoi

When the antecedent is not a noun but a **whole idea**, or is unknown, prefix with **ce**.

| Form | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ce qui** | subject | *Je ne sais pas **ce qui** se passe.* |
| **ce que** | direct object | *Dis-moi **ce que** tu veux.* |
| **ce dont** | with *de* | *C'est **ce dont** j'ai besoin.* |
| **ce à quoi** | with *à* | *C'est **ce à quoi** je pense.* |
| **ce pour quoi**, etc. | other prepositions | *Voilà **ce pour quoi** je me bats.* |

::: examples
Ce qui m'énerve, c'est le bruit. || What annoys me is the noise.
Je comprends ce que tu dis. || I understand what you're saying.
Ce dont j'ai peur, c'est de tomber. || What I'm afraid of is falling.
Ce à quoi je m'attendais. || What I was expecting.
:::

### Commenting on a whole clause

**ce qui** and **ce que** also refer back to an entire preceding statement — English *which*:

::: examples
Il est parti, ce qui m'a surpris. || He left, which surprised me.
Elle a réussi, ce que personne n'attendait. || …which nobody expected.
Il pleut, ce qui est normal en novembre. ||
:::

::: warning A comma plus qui is not the same
*Il est parti, **qui** m'a surpris* is wrong. When the antecedent is the whole idea rather than
a noun, you must use **ce qui**.
:::

### tout ce qui / tout ce que

::: examples
Tout ce qui brille n'est pas or. || All that glitters is not gold. — subject
Tout ce que je sais. || Everything I know. — object
Tout ce dont j'ai besoin. || Everything I need.
:::

## celui qui, celui que

Combining the demonstrative pronoun with a relative gives English *the one who / that*.

::: examples
Celui qui parle est mon frère. || The one who's speaking…
Prends celle que tu veux. || Take the one you want.
Ceux qui arrivent en retard attendront. || Those who arrive late…
C'est celui dont je t'ai parlé. || It's the one I told you about.
:::

## Choosing, in one flowchart

1. Is there **no noun antecedent** — just an idea? → **ce qui / ce que / ce dont / ce à quoi**
2. Is the preposition **de**, plain? → **dont**
3. Is it a **place or time**? → **où**
4. Is there another preposition, and a **person**? → **qui**
5. Is there another preposition, and a **thing**? → **lequel** and its forms
6. No preposition — subject? → **qui**. Object? → **que**
