# Pairs that catch everyone

A reference chapter. These are the distinctions that survive fluency — the ones that still trip
people up after years, because English merges them and there is no rule to derive, only a
habit to build.

## c'est vs il est

The single most common source of error at every level.

| Use **c'est** | Use **il / elle est** |
|---|---|
| + article + noun: *C'est **un** médecin.* | + bare profession: *Il est médecin.* |
| + name: *C'est Marie.* | + adjective about a person: *Elle est grande.* |
| + stressed pronoun: *C'est moi.* | + adjective already introduced: *Ce livre ? Il est bon.* |
| + general comment: *C'est difficile.* | |
| + superlative: *C'est le meilleur.* | |

::: rule The article is the trigger
If a determiner (*un, le, mon, ce*) stands before the noun, use **c'est**. If the noun is bare —
which in French happens with professions, nationalities and religions — use **il est**.

*Il est **professeur**.* — bare noun.
*C'est **un** professeur.* — with an article.
*C'est **un bon** professeur.* — an adjective forces the article, so *c'est*.
:::

::: warning The adjective test
*C'est difficile* — commenting on a situation in general.
*Elle est difficile* — describing a specific person or thing.

For the "it" of an unspecified situation, French uses **c'est** with a masculine singular
adjective: *C'est **bon***, *c'est **fini***, *c'est **vrai***.
:::

::: examples
C'est difficile de parler français. || Introducing an idea: *c'est* + adjective + *de* + infinitive.
Parler français, c'est difficile. || Same thing, idea first.
Il est difficile de parler français. || Formal written variant of the first.
:::

## savoir vs connaître

Both mean *to know*.

| **savoir** | **connaître** |
|---|---|
| facts, information | people, places, works of art |
| + a clause: *Je sais **qu'il vient**.* | + a noun: *Je connais **Paris**.* |
| + a question word: *Je sais **où** il est.* | never followed by a clause |
| + infinitive = a skill: *Je sais **nager**.* | |

::: examples
Je sais qu'il vient. || a fact
Je connais Marie. || a person
Je sais nager. || a skill
Je connais cette chanson. || a familiar thing
Tu sais son nom ? — Oui, mais je ne le connais pas. || I know his name, but I don't know him.
:::

Remember from Part II that these change meaning in the passé composé: *j'ai su* = I found out,
*j'ai connu* = I met.

## an vs année (and jour / journée, soir / soirée, matin / matinée)

| Short form | Long form |
|---|---|
| a **unit**, counted | a **duration**, experienced |
| *j'ai trente **ans*** | *une bonne **année*** |
| *trois **jours*** | *toute la **journée*** |
| *ce **soir*** | *une belle **soirée*** |

::: rule Counted → short. Described → long.
With a **number**, use the short form: *deux ans*, *trois jours*, *cinq matins*.
With an **adjective** or a sense of the whole span, use the long one: *une longue journée*,
*toute l'année*, *une bonne soirée*.

*Bonne **année*** (happy new year), *bonne **journée*** (have a good day), *bonne **soirée***
(have a good evening) — all long forms, because they describe the span.
:::

::: examples
J'ai vingt-cinq ans. || counted
Bonne année ! || described
J'ai travaillé toute la journée. || the whole span
Je pars dans trois jours. || counted
:::

## amener / emmener / apporter / emporter

Four verbs where English uses *bring* and *take*. Two axes: **people vs things**, and
**towards vs away**.

| | Towards (bring) | Away (take) |
|---|---|---|
| **People** | amener | emmener |
| **Things** | apporter | emporter |

::: examples
J'amène mon frère à la fête. || I'm bringing my brother.
J'emmène mon frère au cinéma. || I'm taking my brother.
J'apporte le gâteau. || I'm bringing the cake.
Emporte ton parapluie. || Take your umbrella with you.
:::

::: note Spoken French blurs this
Many native speakers use *amener* for things too. It is a well-known relaxation of the rule, and
still marked as incorrect in careful writing.
:::

## manquer

Reverses the subject and object relative to English.

::: examples
Tu me manques. || I miss you — literally *you are lacking to me*.
Je te manque. || You miss me.
Ma famille me manque. || I miss my family.
Il me manque dix euros. || I'm ten euros short.
:::

::: warning The person doing the missing is the indirect object
*I miss you* is ***tu** me manques*, with **tu** as the subject. Saying *je te manque* means the
opposite. Reason it as *you are missed by me*.
:::

## plaire

Works the same way, and is the natural way to say you like something:

::: examples
Ça me plaît. || I like that.
Ce film m'a beaucoup plu. || I really liked that film.
Tu lui plais. || He / she likes you.
:::

## Other pairs worth fixing

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| **entendre** / **écouter** | to hear (passive) / to listen (deliberate) |
| **voir** / **regarder** | to see / to watch |
| **rentrer** / **retourner** / **revenir** | go home / go back there / come back here |
| **visiter** / **rendre visite à** | visit a place / visit a person |
| **quitter** / **partir** / **laisser** | leave someone or somewhere (needs an object) / leave (no object) / leave something behind |
| **penser à** / **penser de** | think about / have an opinion of |
| **jouer à** / **jouer de** | play a game / play an instrument |
| **matin** / **midi** / **soir** | morning / noon / evening |
| **poser une question** | to ask a question — never *demander une question* |
| **assister à** | to attend, not to assist |
| **attendre** | to wait for — no preposition |
| **demander** | to ask for — no preposition |

::: examples
Je visite Paris. || a place
Je rends visite à ma tante. || a person
Je quitte la maison à 8h. || *quitter* needs an object
Je pars à 8h. || *partir* takes none
Qu'est-ce que tu penses de ce film ? || asking an opinion
Je pense à toi. || thinking about someone
Je joue au tennis. || a game
Je joue du piano. || an instrument
:::

## False friends

| French | Actually means | English word is |
|---|---|---|
| **actuellement** | currently | *actually* = en fait |
| **éventuellement** | possibly | *eventually* = finalement |
| **assister à** | to attend | *to assist* = aider |
| **rester** | to stay | *to rest* = se reposer |
| **la lecture** | reading | *lecture* = un cours, une conférence |
| **la librairie** | bookshop | *library* = la bibliothèque |
| **la monnaie** | change (coins) | *money* = l'argent |
| **sensible** | sensitive | *sensible* = raisonnable |
| **la journée** | the day | *journey* = le voyage |
| **blesser** | to injure | *to bless* = bénir |
| **demander** | to ask | *to demand* = exiger |
| **passer un examen** | to sit an exam | *to pass* = réussir un examen |
| **le chat** | cat | *to chat* = bavarder |
| **la veste** | jacket | *vest* = le maillot de corps |
| **le préservatif** | condom | *preservative* = le conservateur |

::: warning Two of these can genuinely embarrass you
*Je suis excité* does not mean *I'm excited* — it means sexually aroused. Say *j'ai hâte* or
*je suis impatient*.

*Je suis plein* does not mean *I'm full* after a meal — it means drunk (or pregnant, of an
animal). Say *j'ai bien mangé* or *je n'ai plus faim*.
:::
