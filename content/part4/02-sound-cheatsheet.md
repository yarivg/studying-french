# Pronunciation cheat sheet

Everything from the four pronunciation chapters, compressed onto one page. Print it, or keep it
open while you read aloud.

## Vowels

| Written | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a, à, â | /a/ | *la table*, *là*, *la tâche* |
| é, -er, -ez, -ai (verb) | /e/ | *l'été*, *parler*, *parlez*, *je parlai* |
| è, ê, ai, ei, aî, -ais, -ait, -aient | /ɛ/ | *le père*, *la fête*, *le lait*, *la reine* |
| i, î, y | /i/ | *le lit*, *l'île*, *le style* |
| o (closed syllable) | /ɔ/ | *la porte*, *le sol* |
| o (open), ô, au, eau | /o/ | *le mot*, *tôt*, *faux*, *l'eau* |
| ou, où, oû | /u/ | *vous*, *où*, *le goût* |
| u, û | **/y/** | *la rue*, *sûr* |
| eu, œu (open) | /ø/ | *deux*, *peu*, *joyeux* |
| eu, œu (before a consonant) | /œ/ | *la peur*, *la sœur*, *le bœuf* |
| e (unstressed) | /ə/ or dropped | *le*, *je*, *maintenant* |

## Nasals

| Written | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| an, am, en, em | /ɑ̃/ | *un an*, *la chambre*, *le vent*, *ensemble* |
| on, om | /ɔ̃/ | *le nom*, *compter* |
| in, im, ain, aim, ein, yn, ym | /ɛ̃/ | *le train*, *la faim*, *plein* |
| un, um | /œ̃/ → often /ɛ̃/ | *brun*, *le parfum* |
| ien | /jɛ̃/ | *bien*, *le chien* |
| oin | /wɛ̃/ | *le coin*, *loin* |

::: rule The nasal switches off if the m/n is doubled or a vowel follows
*bon* /bɔ̃/ → *bonne* /bɔn/ · *fin* → *fine* · *l'an* → *l'année* · *parfum* → *parfumer*
:::

## Glides

| Written | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| oi, oy | /wa/ | *le roi*, *royal* |
| ui | /ɥi/ | *la nuit*, *la cuisine* |
| ou + vowel | /w/ | *oui*, *le jouet* |
| i + vowel | /j/ | *dieu*, *le pied* |
| ill, -aille, -eille, -ouille | /j/ | *la famille*, *le soleil*, *la grenouille* |
| -ail, -aille | /aj/ | *le travail*, *la bataille* |
| -eil, -eille | /ɛj/ | *le soleil*, *la bouteille* |
| -euil, -ueil | /œj/ | *le portefeuille*, *l'accueil* |

*mille* · *la ville* · *tranquille* · *le village* — all /il/, not /ij/.
:::

## Silent letters

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| final consonant, by default | *le banc*, *trop*, *le prix* |
| **but** c, r, f, l often survive — CaReFuL | *le parc*, *sur*, *neuf*, *le sel* |
| final **-e** | *grande*, *la balade* |
| final **-s / -x** (plural) | *les chiens*, *les gâteaux* |
| verb ending **-ent** | *ils chantent* |
| **-er** infinitive: the r | *parler* /paʁle/ |
| the **u** in gu-, qu- | *la guitare*, *que* |
| **h**, always | *l'homme* |

::: warning -ent is silent on verbs only
*ils parl**ent*** /paʁl/ — silent.
*souv**ent***, *le v**ent***, *cont**ent*** — nasal /ɑ̃/, pronounced.
:::

## Liaisons

| Final letter | Becomes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| s, x, z | /z/ | *vous avez*, *deux ans*, *chez elle* |
| d, t | /t/ | *grand arbre*, *quand il* |
| n | /n/ | *mon avis*, *en avion* |
| f | /v/ | *neuf ans*, *neuf heures* |
| q | /k/ | *cinq ans* |
| r | /ʁ/ | *premier enfant* |
| p | /p/ | *trop aimable* |

**Obligatory:** article + noun · number + noun · adjective + noun · pronoun + verb ·
verb + pronoun (inverted) · pronoun + pronoun · short adverb or preposition + word.

**Forbidden:** after **et** · before **h aspiré** · after a singular noun · before *onze*,
*oui* · across a pause.

## The -emment rule

Adverbs from adjectives in **-ent** are written **-emment** and pronounced **-amment** /amɑ̃/.

*évidemment* · *fréquemment* · *récemment* · *patiemment*

They rhyme exactly with the **-amment** family: *constamment*, *couramment*.

## The five sounds English speakers must build

1. **/y/** — *u*. Say *ee*, keep the tongue there, round the lips. *rue*, *tu*, *sur*.
2. **/ʁ/** — *r*. Back of the throat, air still flowing. *rue*, *Paris*, *quatre*.
3. **The nasals** — air through the nose, and the n or m is **not** pronounced. *vin*, *vent*,
   *vont*.
4. **/ø/ and /œ/** — *eu*. Say *e*, round the lips. *deux*, *peur*.
5. **No diphthongs.** French vowels are pure and steady. English *day* glides from /e/ to /ɪ/;
   French *dé* does not move at all.

::: tip One habit that fixes half of it
French puts roughly **equal length and stress on every syllable**, with a slight lengthening on
the last one of a phrase. English does the opposite — one strong beat and several weak ones.

Reading aloud with deliberately even syllables, even if it feels robotic, gets you closer to
sounding French than any individual sound does.
:::
