# Liaisons

The previous chapter said final consonants are silent. Here is the twist: when the next word
begins with a vowel, that silent consonant **wakes up and attaches to the next word**. This is
*la liaison*, and it is the main reason spoken French sounds like a continuous stream rather
than a sequence of words.

::: examples
vous avez || said *vou-zavez* — the silent **s** becomes /z/
deux ans || said *deu-zans*
mon avis || said *mon-navis*
grand arbre || said *gran-tarbre*
:::

## Which consonant makes which sound

The liaison consonant does not always keep its own value. Three of them change.

| Final letter | Liaison sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **s, x, z** | /z/ | *vous avez*, *deux ans*, *chez elle*, *les amis*, *dix euros* |
| **d, t** | /t/ | *grand arbre*, *petit insecte*, *quand il*, *un grand homme* |
| **n** | /n/ | *mon avis*, *un an*, *en avion*, *bien entendu* |
| **f** | /v/ | *neuf ans*, *neuf heures* |
| **q** | /k/ | *cinq ans*, *cinq euros* |
| **r** | /ʁ/ | *premier enfant*, *le dernier étage* |
| **p** | /p/ | *trop aimable*, *beaucoup aimé* |

::: warning d becomes t, f becomes v
These two catch everyone. *Un grand homme* is /gʁɑ̃tɔm/, not /gʁɑ̃dɔm/. *Neuf ans* is
/nœvɑ̃/, not /nœfɑ̃/ — and yet *neuf euros* is also /nœvøʁo/, while *neuf* before a consonant
stays /nœf/.

**f → v** happens with only two words in practice: *neuf ans* and *neuf heures*.
:::

## When you must make the liaison

Liaison is obligatory inside a tight grammatical unit — roughly, wherever the two words belong
to the same phrase and you would not pause between them.

| Between | Example |
|---|---|
| article + noun | *les**_**amis*, *un**_**enfant*, *des**_**arbres* |
| number + noun | *deux**_**ans*, *trois**_**heures*, *cinq**_**euros* |
| adjective + noun | *un grand**_**arbre*, *les petits**_**enfants*, *un bon**_**ami* |
| pronoun + verb | *nous**_**allons*, *vous**_**êtes*, *ils**_**ont*, *on**_**arrive* |
| verb + pronoun (inverted) | *ont-ils*, *va-t-il*, *sont-elles* |
| pronoun + pronoun | *vous**_**en avez*, *nous**_**y allons* |
| short adverb / preposition + word | *très**_**important*, *chez**_**elle*, *dans**_**une heure*, *en**_**avion* |

::: rule The plural test
This is where liaison earns its keep. *Un ami* /œ̃nami/ and *des amis* /dezami/ differ only in
the liaison consonant. Without it, singular and plural sound the same and the sentence becomes
ambiguous.
:::

## When you must not

| Never link | Why | Example |
|---|---|---|
| after **et** | fixed rule, no exceptions | *un homme et_ une femme* — never *et-t-une* |
| before **h aspiré** | it behaves like a consonant | *les / hobbies*, *en / haut*, *les / héros* |
| after a singular noun | the noun ends its own phrase | *un enfant / intelligent* |
| before **onze**, **oui** | treated as h aspiré | *les / onze joueurs*, *un / oui franc* |
| across a comma or pause | punctuation blocks it | *Alors, / on y va ?* |

::: warning et never links
*Et* ends in a **t**, so learners naturally want to link it. French never does.
*Paul et Anne* is /pɔl e an/, with a clean break. This one mistake is unusually noticeable.
:::

## Optional liaisons

A large middle group is optional, and the choice signals register. Making more liaisons sounds
more formal, careful or literary; making fewer sounds relaxed and modern.

::: examples
je suis allé || careful: *sui-zallé* // casual: *je sui allé*
pas encore || careful: *pa-zencore* // casual: *pa encore*
après avoir mangé || careful: *aprè-zavoir* // casual: no link
:::

As a learner, follow the obligatory ones and let the optional ones come naturally. Over-liaising
sounds stranger than under-liaising.

## Enchaînement: the quieter cousin

Liaison wakes a *silent* consonant. **Enchaînement** simply carries an *already pronounced*
consonant onto the next word. It happens constantly and you do not have to think about it.

::: examples
il arrive || *i-larrive* — the l was always pronounced
quelle heure est-il ? || *que-lheu-rétil*
une autre idée || *u-nau-tridée*
:::

The effect is that French syllables tend to end in vowels, which is why the language sounds so
smooth compared with English's consonant-heavy word endings.

## Elision

Related, and purely written: a few short words drop their vowel before another vowel and take
an apostrophe.

| Full form | Becomes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| le, la | **l'** | *l'ami*, *l'école*, *l'heure* |
| je | **j'** | *j'ai*, *j'habite* |
| ne | **n'** | *je n'ai pas* |
| de | **d'** | *d'accord*, *beaucoup d'eau* |
| que | **qu'** | *qu'il*, *qu'est-ce que* |
| se | **s'** | *s'appeler*, *il s'habille* |
| ce | **c'** | *c'est* |
| me, te | **m'**, **t'** | *il m'aime*, *je t'appelle* |
| si | **s'** (only before *il*, *ils*) | *s'il vous plaît* — but *si elle* stays |

::: warning si only elides before il
*S'il vous plaît* and *s'ils viennent*, but *si elle vient* and *si on part* keep the full *si*.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Link or not?
*les amis* || Link. Article + noun is obligatory. /lezami/
*et elle* || No link. Never after **et**.
*un grand homme* || Link, with **d** → /t/. /gʁɑ̃tɔm/
*les hobbies* || No link. *Hobby* has h aspiré.
*neuf heures* || Link, with **f** → /v/. /nœvœʁ/
*un enfant intelligent* || No link. A singular noun does not link to the adjective after it.
*nous allons* || Link. Pronoun + verb is obligatory. /nuzalɔ̃/
*cinq ans* || Link, **q** → /k/. /sɛ̃kɑ̃/
*chez elle* || Link, **z** → /z/. /ʃezɛl/
*les onze joueurs* || No link. *Onze* blocks liaison like an h aspiré.
:::
