# Vowel sounds

French spelling is far more consistent than English spelling — it just uses different
conventions. Almost every vowel combination has exactly one sound. Learn the twenty or so
patterns below and you can pronounce a word you have never seen.

::: sound You can hear all of this
The phonetic symbols are a shorthand, not the point. Click any French example on the page and
the browser reads it aloud; every transcription like /pwasɔ̃/ has a play button beside it that
speaks the word it describes. Shift-click for a slow reading. The speaker in the header turns
the sound off or changes voice.
:::

Two ideas matter more than any single rule:

1. **Combinations beat letters.** `ai`, `ou`, `eau` are single vowels that happen to be
   written with several letters. Never sound them out one letter at a time.
2. **Accents are not stress marks.** French has no word stress the way English does. An accent
   either changes the vowel's sound (`é`, `è`) or distinguishes two spellings (`ou` / `où`).

## Accented vowels

| Written | Sound | Notes | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **é** | /e/ | Accent aigu. Tight, closed — the "ay" of "day" with no glide at the end. | *l'été*, *le café*, *le thé*, *le marché* |
| **è** | /ɛ/ | Accent grave. Open — the vowel of English "fair". | *le père*, *la mère*, *la bière*, *le frère* |
| **ê** | /ɛ/ | Accent circonflexe. Same as **è**. | *la fête*, *la tête*, *le rêve*, *la forêt* |
| **à** | /a/ | Same sound as plain **a**; the accent only separates *à* (to) from *a* (has). | *là*, *déjà*, *voilà* |
| **â** | /ɑ/ | A darker, longer **a**. In most modern speech it has merged with /a/. | *la tâche*, *l'âme*, *le château*, *gâcher* |
| **î** | /i/ | Same as plain **i**. | *l'île*, *le dîner*, *la boîte* |
| **ô** | /o/ | Closed, rounded — like **eau**. | *tôt*, *aussitôt*, *drôle*, *le pôle* |
| **û** | /y/ | Same as plain **u**. | *sûrement*, *la piqûre* |
| **ù** | /u/ | Appears in exactly one common word. | *où* |

::: note The circumflex is a fossil
A circumflex usually marks an **s** that fell out of the word centuries ago. That is why
*la forêt* is *forest*, *l'île* is *isle*, *le château* is *castle*, and *hâte* is *haste*.
When you see one, try putting an s back — the English cognate often appears.
:::

## The tréma

A **tréma** (¨) means *pronounce this vowel separately* — it breaks up a combination that
would otherwise fuse into one sound.

| Written | Without tréma it would be | With tréma | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ï** | "ai" = /ɛ/ | two sounds: a‑i | *maïs* = ma‑ees (not *mais*) |
| **ë** | "oe" = one vowel | two sounds | *Noël* = no‑el |
| **ü** | "gu" = /g/ | the u is heard | *aiguë* |

::: warning maïs vs mais
*mais* (but) is one syllable, /mɛ/. *maïs* (corn) is two, /ma.is/. The tréma is doing all the
work — it is the only thing that separates them.
:::

## Vowel combinations

These are the ones worth drilling, because they never say what an English reader expects.

| Written | Sound | Say it like | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ai, ei** | /ɛ/ | the "e" of "fair" | *le lait*, *le fait*, *la reine*, *la neige* |
| **aî** | /ɛ/ | same as **ai** | *frais / fraîche*, *le maître*, *connaître* |
| **au, eau** | /o/ | closed *oh*, lips rounded | *le gâteau*, *l'eau*, *beau*, *faux*, *la sauce* |
| **ou** | /u/ | the "oo" of "food" | *vous*, *la soupe*, *la roue*, *le loup* |
| **u** | /y/ | **not** "oo" — see below | *la rue*, *le tube*, *sur*, *la culture* |
| **oi** | /wa/ | "wah" | *le roi*, *la joie*, *le mois*, *le doigt* |
| **ui** | /ɥi/ | "wee" with rounded lips | *la nuit*, *fuir*, *la cuisine* |
| **eu, œu** (open syllable) | /ø/ | rounded "uh" | *eux*, *peu*, *deux*, *joyeux* |
| **eu, œu** (before a consonant) | /œ/ | more open, jaw lower | *la peur*, *l'heure*, *le bœuf*, *la sœur* |
| **ieu** | /jø/ | "y" + the /ø/ above | *dieu*, *le milieu* |
| **oy, ay** | /waj/, /ɛj/ | the y acts as two i's | *royal*, *payer*, *essayer* |

::: warning The single most important vowel: u vs ou
English has no /y/, so **u** is the sound most likely to give you away.

- **ou** = /u/. Round your lips and say *oo*. Tongue at the **back**.
- **u** = /y/. Say *ee*, hold your tongue exactly there, then round your lips. Tongue at the **front**.

Minimal pairs to practise: *dessous* (underneath) vs *dessus* (on top) · *la roue* (wheel) vs
*la rue* (street) · *tout* (all) vs *tu* (you) · *pour* (for) vs *pur* (pure).
:::

## Nasal vowels

A vowel followed by **m** or **n** becomes nasal — the air goes through your nose and the
consonant itself **disappears**. You never actually pronounce the n in *vin*.

The nasal is cancelled if the **m/n is doubled** or **followed by a vowel**. Compare
*bon* /bɔ̃/ with *bonne* /bɔn/.

| Written | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **an, am, en, em, aon** | /ɑ̃/ | *un an*, *le vent*, *la chambre*, *content*, *novembre*, *ensemble* |
| **on, om** | /ɔ̃/ | *le nom*, *la chanson*, *le pont*, *compter*, *le savon* |
| **in, im, ain, aim, ein, yn, ym** | /ɛ̃/ | *le train*, *plein*, *la faim*, *le pain*, *le jardin*, *vilain* |
| **un, um** | /œ̃/ | *brun*, *le parfum*, *un*, *lundi* |
| **ien** | /jɛ̃/ | *bien*, *le chien*, *rien*, *le lien* |
| **oin** | /wɛ̃/ | *le coin*, *loin*, *le point*, *le besoin* |

::: tip /ɛ̃/ and /œ̃/ are merging
In Paris and most of northern France, *brun* and *brin* now sound identical — both /ɛ̃/.
Keeping them apart marks you as southern or old-fashioned, not as wrong. If you find /œ̃/
hard, use /ɛ̃/ everywhere and no one will blink.
:::

::: warning When the nasal switches off
Doubling the consonant, or adding a vowel after it, restores the ordinary vowel **and** makes
the n audible.

- *bon* /bɔ̃/ → *bonne* /bɔn/
- *le parfum* /paʁfœ̃/ → *parfumer* /paʁfyme/
- *fin* /fɛ̃/ → *fine* /fin/
- *le frein* /fʁɛ̃/ → *freiner* /fʁɛne/
- *l'an* /ɑ̃/ → *l'année* /ane/
- *inoubliable* — the *in-* here is /in/, because a vowel follows.
:::

## The vowel that hides: -ille

The letters **ill** usually make a /j/ sound, like English *y*.

| Written | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-ille** | /ij/ | *la famille*, *la fille*, *la bouteille* |
| **-aille** | /aj/ | *la bataille*, *la paille*, *travailler* |
| **-ail** | /aj/ | *le travail*, *l'ail* |
| **-eil, -eille** | /ɛj/ | *le soleil*, *pareil*, *la bouteille* |
| **-euil, -euille, -ueil** | /œj/ | *le portefeuille*, *l'accueil*, *l'orgueil*, *l'écureuil* |
| **-ouille** | /uj/ | *les nouilles*, *la grenouille* |

::: warning Four words break the -ille rule
In these, **ill** is simply /il/ — no /j/ at all:

*mille* (thousand) · *la ville* (city) · *tranquille* (calm) · *le village*

So *la fille* is /fij/ but *la ville* is /vil/. There is no logic; they are just old.
:::

## A rule that only affects adverbs

The ending **-emment** is pronounced **-amment** /amɑ̃/, exactly like **-amment**.

::: examples
évidemment || obviously — say *évidamment*
fréquemment || frequently — say *fréquamment*
récemment || recently — say *récamment*
:::

This matters because a whole family of adverbs is built this way: any adjective ending in
**-ent** makes its adverb in **-emment**, and every one of them sounds like *-amment*.

## Practice

::: exercise Which sound?
*le gâteau* — what sound does **eau** make? || /o/ — closed, rounded, like **ô**
*la nuit* — **ui** is one sound or two? || One: /ɥi/, a rounded "wee"
*maïs* — how many syllables? || Two: ma‑ees. The tréma splits *ai* apart.
*bonne* — is the vowel nasal? || No. The doubled **n** cancels the nasal and is pronounced.
*la ville* — /vij/ or /vil/? || /vil/ — one of the four exceptions to the -ille rule.
*évidemment* — which vowel in the ending? || /a/ — **-emment** is said **-amment**.
*la rue* vs *la roue* — what is the difference? || *rue* = /y/ (tongue forward, lips round); *roue* = /u/ (tongue back).
*le frein* → *freiner* — does the nasal survive? || No. A vowel follows the n, so *freiner* is /fʁɛne/.
:::

le portefeuille || /pɔʁtəfœj/ — **euille** = /œj/
le poisson || /pwasɔ̃/ — **oi** = /wa/, **on** = nasal /ɔ̃/
l'écureuil || /ekyʁœj/ — note the **u** /y/ in the middle
aussitôt || /osito/ — **au** and **ô** are the same /o/
la bouteille || /butɛj/ — **ou** = /u/, **eille** = /ɛj/
le champignon || /ʃɑ̃piɲɔ̃/ — two nasals, and **gn** = /ɲ/
:::
