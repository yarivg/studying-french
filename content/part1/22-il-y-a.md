# Il y a

**Il y a** means *there is* and *there are* — one form for both. It is frozen: the *il* is not a
real subject, and the phrase never agrees with what follows.

::: examples
Il y a un problème. || There is a problem.
Il y a des enfants dans le jardin. || There are children in the garden.
Il y a beaucoup de monde. || There are a lot of people.
:::

::: rule Never *ils y ont*
Singular or plural, the form is identical. *Il y a un livre* and *il y a **dix** livres* use the
same words. Nothing agrees with anything.
:::

## Across the tenses

Only the *avoir* part changes.

| Tense | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | il y a | *Il y a du monde.* |
| **Imperfect** | il y avait | *Il y avait du monde.* — there was / there used to be |
| **Passé composé** | il y a eu | *Il y a eu un accident.* — there was (one event) |
| **Future** | il y aura | *Il y aura du monde.* |
| **Conditional** | il y aurait | *Il y aurait un problème.* — apparently there's a problem |
| **Subjunctive** | qu'il y ait | *Je doute qu'il y ait du monde.* |
| **With devoir / pouvoir** | il doit y avoir, il peut y avoir | *Il doit y avoir une erreur.* |

::: warning il y avait vs il y a eu
*Il y **avait** beaucoup de monde.* — describing a scene, an ongoing state.
*Il y **a eu** un accident.* — a single event that happened.

The same imperfect-versus-passé-composé logic as any other verb, covered fully in Part II.
:::

## Negative and question forms

::: examples
Il n'y a pas de problème. || There's no problem — *un* collapses to *de*.
Il n'y a personne. || There's nobody.
Il n'y a rien. || There's nothing.
Il n'y avait plus de pain. || There was no more bread.
Y a-t-il un problème ? || Is there a problem? — formal inversion
Est-ce qu'il y a un problème ? || neutral
Il y a un problème ? || spoken
:::

::: note In speech, il y a becomes y a
*Il y a* is almost always reduced to /ja/ in conversation, and the negative *il n'y a pas de* to
/japad/.

- *Y a un problème ?*
- *Y a personne.*
- *Y a pas de quoi.* — you're welcome.

Understand it; write the full form.
:::

## il y a = ago

The same three words, followed by a **duration**, mean *ago*.

::: examples
Il est parti il y a une heure. || He left an hour ago.
Je l'ai vu il y a deux jours. || I saw him two days ago.
Il y a longtemps. || A long time ago.
:::

::: warning il y a vs depuis — the direction of time
***Il y a** deux ans, j'ai déménagé.* — two years **ago** I moved. A finished point in the past.
***Depuis** deux ans, j'habite ici.* — **for** two years I've lived here. Still true now.

The tense gives it away: *il y a* takes a past tense, *depuis* takes the **present**.
:::

And a third pattern, **il y a … que**, which behaves like *depuis*:

::: examples
Il y a deux ans que j'habite ici. || I've lived here for two years.
Ça fait deux ans que j'habite ici. || Same meaning, more common in speech.
:::

All three ways of saying it: *J'habite ici **depuis** deux ans* = *Il y a deux ans **que**
j'habite ici* = *Ça fait deux ans **que** j'habite ici.*

## Related expressions

| Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **voilà** / **voici** | there is / here is — pointing at something |
| **c'est** / **ce sont** | it is / these are — identifying |
| **il reste** | there is … left |
| **il manque** | there is … missing |
| **il faut** | it is necessary, one must |
| **il s'agit de** | it is about |

::: examples
Voilà le livre ! || There's the book — pointing at it.
Il reste du pain. || There's some bread left.
Il manque une chaise. || There's a chair missing.
Il faut partir. || We have to leave.
De quoi s'agit-il ? || What's it about?
:::

::: warning il y a vs voilà
**Il y a** states that something exists. **Voilà** points at it.

*Il y a un restaurant dans la rue.* — there is one (information).
*Voilà le restaurant !* — there it is (pointing).
:::

::: warning il y a vs c'est
**Il y a** introduces something new. **C'est** identifies something already in view.

*Il y a un homme à la porte.* — there's a man at the door.
*C'est mon frère.* — it's my brother.

A natural sequence uses both: *Il y a quelqu'un ? — Oui, c'est moi.*
:::

## il faut

Worth a note here because it is built the same impersonal way. **Il faut** means *it is
necessary* and takes an infinitive or a noun.

::: examples
Il faut partir. || We have to leave / one must leave.
Il faut du courage. || It takes courage.
Il faudra attendre. || We'll have to wait.
Il fallait le dire ! || You should have said so!
Il ne faut pas fumer ici. || You mustn't smoke here.
:::

::: warning il ne faut pas means "must not", not "need not"
*Il ne faut pas fumer* = you **must not** smoke, a prohibition.
For *you don't have to*, say *tu n'es pas obligé de* or *ce n'est pas nécessaire de*.
:::

To name the person, use **il faut que** + subjunctive (Part II) or simply an indirect pronoun:
*Il **me** faut du temps* — I need time.
