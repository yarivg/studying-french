# Numbers

French counting is regular up to sixty, and then does something famously strange. Everything
in this chapter is memorisation, but the memorisation is finite.

## 0 to 20

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 zéro | 6 six | 11 onze | 16 seize |
| 1 un / une | 7 sept | 12 douze | 17 dix-sept |
| 2 deux | 8 huit | 13 treize | 18 dix-huit |
| 3 trois | 9 neuf | 14 quatorze | 19 dix-neuf |
| 4 quatre | 10 dix | 15 quinze | 20 vingt |
| 5 cinq | | | |

::: warning un is the only number with a gender
*un livre* but *une table*. *Vingt et un ans* but *vingt et une minutes*. Every other number
is invariable.
:::

## The tens

| | |
|---|---|
| 20 vingt | 70 soixante-dix |
| 30 trente | 80 quatre-vingts |
| 40 quarante | 90 quatre-vingt-dix |
| 50 cinquante | 100 cent |
| 60 soixante | |

From 70 up, French switches to arithmetic:

- **70** = *soixante-dix* — sixty-ten
- **71** = *soixante et onze* — sixty and eleven
- **75** = *soixante-quinze* — sixty-fifteen
- **80** = *quatre-vingts* — four twenties
- **85** = *quatre-vingt-cinq* — four-twenty-five
- **90** = *quatre-vingt-dix* — four-twenty-ten
- **97** = *quatre-vingt-dix-sept* — four-twenty-seventeen

::: tip Belgium and Switzerland fixed this
*septante* (70), *nonante* (90), and in parts of Switzerland *huitante* (80). They are
perfectly good French and much easier. In France they will be understood but sound foreign, so
learn the French forms for production and the Belgian ones for listening.
:::

## Joining the numbers

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, 71 use **et**, with no hyphen | *vingt et un*, *soixante et onze* |
| 81 and 91 do **not** use *et* | *quatre-vingt-un*, *quatre-vingt-onze* |
| everything else takes a hyphen | *vingt-deux*, *quatre-vingt-dix-sept* |

::: note The 1990 spelling reform
Modern practice hyphenates every part of a compound number: *vingt-et-un*, *deux-cent-trente*.
Both the old and new conventions are accepted. Nothing about the pronunciation changes.
:::

## Hundreds, thousands, millions

| Number | French | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | cent | no *un* before it |
| 101 | cent un | no *et* |
| 200 | deux cents | takes **-s** |
| 250 | deux cent cinquante | loses the **-s** when another number follows |
| 1 000 | mille | invariable — never *milles* |
| 2 000 | deux mille | still no **-s** |
| 1 000 000 | un million | a noun: takes *un*, and *de* before another noun |
| 1 000 000 000 | un milliard | same |

::: examples
deux cents euros || 200 — with -s
deux cent dix euros || 210 — the -s vanishes
trois mille personnes || 3,000
un million d'habitants || a million inhabitants — note the *de*
:::

::: warning France writes numbers the other way round
A space (or a dot) separates thousands, and a **comma** is the decimal point.

- 1 000 000 or 1.000.000 — one million
- 3,14 — three point one four
- 19,99 € — nineteen euros ninety-nine

Say *dix-neuf euros quatre-vingt-dix-neuf*, or in shops simply *dix-neuf quatre-vingt-dix-neuf*.
:::

## Pronunciation traps

The final consonant of several numbers changes depending on what follows.

| Number | Alone | Before a consonant | Before a vowel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cinq** | /sɛ̃k/ | /sɛ̃/ or /sɛ̃k/ | /sɛ̃k/ — *cinq ans* |
| **six** | /sis/ | /si/ — *six livres* | /siz/ — *six ans* |
| **huit** | /ɥit/ | /ɥi/ — *huit jours* | /ɥit/ — *huit ans* |
| **dix** | /dis/ | /di/ — *dix jours* | /diz/ — *dix ans* |
| **neuf** | /nœf/ | /nœf/ | /nœv/ — *neuf ans*, *neuf heures* |
| **vingt** | /vɛ̃/ | /vɛ̃/ | /vɛ̃t/ — *vingt ans* |

::: examples
J'ai six ans. || /sizɑ̃/ — the x is /z/
J'ai six livres. || /si livʁ/ — the x is silent
Il y en a six. || /sis/ — standing alone, the x is /s/
:::

And in **-ième** forms, the x becomes /z/: *deuxième* /døzjɛm/, *dixième* /dizjɛm*/.

## Ordinals

Add **-ième** to the number. Drop a final **-e** first.

| Cardinal | Ordinal | Written short |
|---|---|---|
| un | **premier / première** | 1er / 1re |
| deux | deuxième (or *second/e*) | 2e |
| trois | troisième | 3e |
| quatre | quatrième | 4e — the *e* is dropped |
| cinq | cinquième | 5e — a *u* is inserted |
| neuf | neuvième | 9e — *f* becomes *v* |
| vingt et un | vingt et unième | 21e |

::: note deuxième or second?
Interchangeable in practice. Purists reserve *second* for a series of exactly two —
*la Seconde Guerre mondiale* — and *deuxième* when more may follow. Nobody will correct you.
:::

## Fractions and approximations

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *le demi*, *la moitié* | half |
| *le tiers* | a third |
| *le quart* | a quarter |
| *les trois quarts* | three quarters |
| *une dizaine* | about ten |
| *une quinzaine* | about fifteen; also *a fortnight* |
| *une centaine* | about a hundred |
| *des milliers* | thousands |
| *environ*, *à peu près* | approximately |

::: examples
une dizaine de personnes || about ten people
la moitié du gâteau || half the cake
environ trente minutes || about thirty minutes
:::
