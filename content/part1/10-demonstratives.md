# This and that

French does not distinguish *this* from *that*. One set of words covers both, and if you
genuinely need to point at one thing rather than another, you bolt on **-ci** or **-là**.

## Demonstrative adjectives: ce, cet, cette, ces

| | Form | Used before | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Masculine singular** | **ce** | a consonant | *ce livre*, *ce chien*, *ce matin* |
| **Masculine singular** | **cet** | a vowel or mute h | *cet arbre*, *cet homme*, *cet été* |
| **Feminine singular** | **cette** | anything | *cette maison*, *cette idée*, *cette heure* |
| **Plural** | **ces** | anything | *ces livres*, *ces maisons*, *ces arbres* |

::: examples
Ce livre est intéressant. || This / that book is interesting.
Cet arbre est très vieux. || This / that tree is very old.
Cette maison est grande. || This / that house is big.
Ces enfants sont fatigués. || These / those children are tired.
:::

::: rule cet and cette sound identical
Both are /sɛt/. The spelling difference is grammatical, not audible — which means you can
speak without deciding, but you cannot write without knowing the gender.
:::

## Making the distinction: -ci and -là

Attach **-ci** (here, nearer) or **-là** (there, further) to the noun with a hyphen.

::: examples
ce livre-ci || this book (here)
ce livre-là || that book (over there)
cette semaine-ci || this week
ce jour-là || that day
:::

::: note In practice, -là does most of the work
Spoken French uses **-là** as a general emphatic marker, even when nothing is far away:
*ce mec-là* (that guy), *à cette époque-là* (at that time). Pure *-ci* is comparatively rare
outside a genuine contrast.
:::

## Demonstrative pronouns: celui, celle, ceux, celles

These replace the noun entirely — English *the one* / *the ones*.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | celui | ceux |
| **Feminine** | celle | celles |

They never stand alone. They must be followed by **-ci / -là**, by **de**, or by a relative
pronoun.

### With -ci and -là

| Gender / number | This one | That one |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine singular | **celui-ci** | **celui-là** |
| Feminine singular | **celle-ci** | **celle-là** |
| Masculine plural | **ceux-ci** | **ceux-là** |
| Feminine plural | **celles-ci** | **celles-là** |

::: examples
Quel livre ? — Celui-là. || Which book? That one.
Quelle robe préfères-tu ? — Celle-ci. || Which dress do you prefer? This one.
Ces chaussures-ci ou celles-là ? || These shoes or those?
:::

### With de — showing possession

::: examples
C'est ma voiture, pas celle de Marie. || It's my car, not Marie's.
Mon avis et celui de mon frère. || My opinion and my brother's.
Les enfants de Paul et ceux de Sophie. || Paul's children and Sophie's.
:::

### With a relative pronoun

::: examples
Celui qui parle est mon frère. || The one who is speaking is my brother.
Prends celle que tu veux. || Take the one you want.
Ceux qui arrivent en retard attendront. || Those who arrive late will wait.
:::

## ce, ceci, cela, ça

A separate set for referring to a whole idea or an unidentified thing, rather than a specific
noun.

| Word | Register | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **ce** | neutral | only before *être*: *c'est*, *ce sont* |
| **ceci** | formal, rare | this (what follows) |
| **cela** | formal / written | that |
| **ça** | everyday spoken | that — the contraction of *cela* |

::: examples
C'est vrai. || That's true.
Ce sont mes amis. || These are my friends.
Ça me plaît. || I like that.
Ça veut dire quoi ? || What does that mean?
Ça y est ! || That's it! / Done!
Ça marche. || That works.
:::

::: warning ça is spoken, cela is written
They mean the same thing. *Ça* dominates conversation to the point that *cela* can sound
stiff; in formal writing the reverse is true. Use *ça* when you speak and you will always
sound natural.
:::

## c'est vs il est

A distinction worth previewing here, because it trips people up constantly.

| Use **c'est** | Use **il / elle est** |
|---|---|
| before a noun with an article: *C'est un médecin.* | before a bare noun of profession: *Il est médecin.* |
| before a name: *C'est Marie.* | before an adjective describing a known person: *Elle est grande.* |
| before a stressed pronoun: *C'est moi.* | |
| for a general comment: *C'est difficile.* | for a specific thing already mentioned: *Ce livre ? Il est difficile.* |

::: examples
C'est un professeur. || He's a teacher — noun with an article
Il est professeur. || He's a teacher — no article before a profession
C'est difficile. || It's difficult — talking about the situation
Elle est difficile. || She's difficult — talking about a person
:::

## Practice

::: exercise ce, cet, cette or ces?
___ livre || ce
___ arbre || cet — masculine before a vowel
___ maison || cette
___ enfants || ces
___ homme || cet — mute h
___ idée || cette
___ été || cet
___ heure || cette — *heure* is feminine
:::

::: exercise Replace with a pronoun
Quel livre ? Le livre là-bas. || Celui-là.
Ma voiture et la voiture de Marie. || Ma voiture et celle de Marie.
Les enfants qui arrivent. || Ceux qui arrivent.
Quelle robe ? La robe ici. || Celle-ci.
Mon avis et l'avis de mon frère. || Mon avis et celui de mon frère.
:::

::: exercise c'est or il / elle est?
___ un bon film. || C'est — a noun with an article
___ médecin. || Il est — a bare profession
___ Marie. || C'est — a name
___ intelligente. || Elle est — an adjective about a person
___ difficile de parler français. || C'est — a general comment
:::
