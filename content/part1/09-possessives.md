# Possessives

The French possessive agrees with **the thing owned**, not with the owner. This is the single
adjustment English speakers have to make, and it takes a while to stop feeling backwards.

## The table

| Owner | Masc. singular | Fem. singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| my | **mon** | **ma** | **mes** |
| your (informal) | **ton** | **ta** | **tes** |
| his / her / its | **son** | **sa** | **ses** |
| our | **notre** | **notre** | **nos** |
| your (formal or plural) | **votre** | **votre** | **vos** |
| their | **leur** | **leur** | **leurs** |

::: rule son livre is "his book" *or* "her book"
French does not mark the owner's gender at all. *Son livre* is his book or her book; *sa
voiture* is his car or her car. Context decides, and if it genuinely matters you add
*à lui* / *à elle*: *C'est sa voiture à elle.*
:::

::: examples
mon frère || my brother — *frère* is masculine
ma sœur || my sister — *sœur* is feminine
mes parents || my parents — plural, gender irrelevant
son père || his father / her father
sa mère || his mother / her mother
leurs enfants || their children
:::

## ma becomes mon before a vowel

Before a feminine noun starting with a vowel or mute h, **ma / ta / sa** become **mon / ton /
son**. This is purely for pronunciation — the noun is still feminine.

::: examples
une amie → mon amie || not *ma amie*
une école → ton école || not *ta école*
une heure → son heure ||
une histoire → mon histoire ||
:::

::: warning The noun is still feminine
*Mon amie* takes feminine adjectives: **mon amie est *belle***, not *beau*. The *mon* is a
pronunciation patch, not a change of gender.

And this only applies when the vowel is immediately after: *ma nouvelle amie* keeps *ma*,
because *nouvelle* now stands between them.
:::

## The body takes le, not mon

With body parts and clothing, French uses the definite article where English uses a
possessive. Ownership is obvious, so it goes unsaid.

::: examples
J'ai mal à la tête. || My head hurts. — literally *I have pain at the head*
Il lève la main. || He raises his hand.
Elle se lave les cheveux. || She washes her hair.
Il met son manteau. || He puts on his coat. — clothing does keep the possessive here
:::

The reflexive pronoun, when there is one, already tells you whose body it is:
*je **me** lave **les** mains* — I wash my hands.

## Emphatic ownership: à moi

To stress who owns something, or to answer *whose?*, French uses **être à** + a stressed
pronoun rather than a possessive.

| Person | Stressed pronoun |
|---|---|
| je | moi |
| tu | toi |
| il / elle | lui / elle |
| nous | nous |
| vous | vous |
| ils / elles | eux / elles |

::: examples
Ce livre est à moi. || This book is mine.
À qui est cette voiture ? — Elle est à Marie. || Whose car is this? It's Marie's.
C'est à toi ? || Is this yours?
:::

## Possessive pronouns: le mien, le tien

To replace the whole noun phrase — English *mine*, *yours*, *hers* — French has a second set.
These agree with the thing owned and always carry an article.

| Owner | Masc. sing. | Fem. sing. | Masc. plural | Fem. plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mine | le mien | la mienne | les miens | les miennes |
| yours (informal) | le tien | la tienne | les tiens | les tiennes |
| his / hers | le sien | la sienne | les siens | les siennes |
| ours | le nôtre | la nôtre | les nôtres | les nôtres |
| yours (formal) | le vôtre | la vôtre | les vôtres | les vôtres |
| theirs | le leur | la leur | les leurs | les leurs |

::: examples
Ma voiture est rouge, la tienne est bleue. || Mine is red, yours is blue.
J'ai oublié mes clés — tu as les tiennes ? || Have you got yours?
Ce n'est pas mon problème, c'est le sien. || It's his / hers.
:::

::: warning notre vs le nôtre
The adjective *notre* has no accent; the pronoun *le nôtre* does, and the vowel changes from
/ɔ/ to /o/. Same for *votre* / *le vôtre*. It is the only thing that distinguishes them in
speech.
:::

## Showing possession between two nouns

There is no French apostrophe-s. Use **de**.

::: examples
le livre de Marie || Marie's book
la voiture de mon père || my father's car
la clé de la maison || the house key
les devoirs des enfants || the children's homework — *de* + *les*
:::

Remember the contractions: *de + le = **du***, *de + les = **des***.
