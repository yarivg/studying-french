# Tout, and how much

**Tout** is one of the most common words in French and one of the least predictable, because
it can be an adjective, a pronoun or an adverb, and the pronunciation shifts with the job.
This chapter also collects every way of saying *how much*.

## The four forms of tout

| Form | Pronunciation | Agrees with | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **tout** | /tu/ | masculine singular | *tout le monde*, *tout le temps*, *tout l'argent* |
| **toute** | /tut/ | feminine singular | *toute la nuit*, *toute la journée*, *toute ma vie* |
| **tous** | /tu/ as adjective, **/tus/** as pronoun | masculine plural | *tous les jours*, *ils sont tous là* |
| **toutes** | /tut/ | feminine plural | *toutes les filles*, *toutes les semaines* |

::: warning The s of tous is only heard when it stands alone
- *tous les jours* → /tu le ʒuʁ/ — the s is **silent**, because *tous* is an adjective before a noun.
- *Ils sont tous là* → /tus/ — the s is **pronounced**, because *tous* is a pronoun standing on its own.

Same spelling, two pronunciations, decided purely by whether a noun follows.
:::

## Tout as an adjective

Placed before the article — *tout* comes first, then *le / la / les / mon / ce*.

| Meaning | Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| the whole of | tout + le/la + singular | *tout le gâteau* (the whole cake), *toute la nuit* (all night) |
| every | tous / toutes + les + plural | *tous les jours* (every day), *toutes les semaines* (every week) |
| any at all | tout + bare noun | *à tout moment* (at any moment), *en tout cas* (in any case) |

::: examples
Tout le monde est là. || Everyone is here — literally *all the world*
J'ai travaillé toute la journée. || I worked all day.
Tous les jours, je prends le métro. || Every day I take the metro.
Toutes les filles sont parties. || All the girls have left.
:::

## Tout as a pronoun

| Form | Means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **tout** | everything | *Tout va bien.* · *C'est tout.* · *Il sait tout.* |
| **tous** /tus/ | all of them (m.) | *Ils sont tous venus.* |
| **toutes** | all of them (f.) | *Elles sont toutes parties.* |

::: examples
Tout est prêt. || Everything is ready.
C'est tout ? || Is that all?
Ils sont tous là. || They're all here — /tus/
Je les ai toutes vues. || I've seen them all.
:::

## Tout as an adverb

Meaning *completely* or *quite*, modifying an adjective or adverb. As an adverb it should be
invariable — but French makes one exception.

::: examples
Il est tout content. || He's really pleased.
Elle est tout étonnée. || She's quite astonished — *tout* stays, because *étonnée* starts with a vowel.
Elles sont toutes contentes. || They're all pleased — agrees, because *contentes* starts with a consonant.
tout à coup || suddenly
tout de suite || right away
tout à fait || absolutely
tout seul || all alone
:::

::: note The one agreement exception
Adverbial *tout* agrees only before a **feminine** adjective beginning with a **consonant**:
*toute contente*, *toutes petites*. Before a vowel it stays *tout*: *tout étonnée*. This exists
purely so the phrase is pronounceable, and it is the sort of rule even native writers check.
:::

## Fixed expressions with tout

| Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| tout le monde | everyone |
| tout de suite | right away |
| tout à coup | suddenly |
| tout à fait | absolutely, completely |
| tout à l'heure | in a little while / a little while ago |
| pas du tout | not at all |
| en tout cas | in any case |
| tout seul / toute seule | all by oneself |
| tous les deux | both |
| tout droit | straight ahead |

::: warning tout à l'heure points both ways
*À tout à l'heure !* means *see you later*. But *Je l'ai vu tout à l'heure* means *I saw him a
little while ago*. The tense of the verb tells you which direction in time.
:::

## Quantifiers: everything takes de

Every expression of quantity is followed by bare **de** — never *du*, *de la* or *des*, and
this holds even when the noun is plural.

| Quantifier | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **beaucoup de** | a lot of | *beaucoup de jardins* |
| **un peu de** | a little | *un peu de sucre* |
| **peu de** | few, not much | *peu de gens* |
| **trop de** | too much / many | *trop de bruit* |
| **assez de** | enough | *assez de temps* |
| **tant de** | so many | *tant de jardins* |
| **autant de** | as much / many | *autant de travail* |
| **plus de** | more | *plus de choix* |
| **moins de** | less, fewer | *moins de gens* |
| **combien de** | how much / many | *combien de temps ?* |
| **la plupart des** | most of | *la plupart des gens* — the exception |

::: rule Never *beaucoup des*
*Beaucoup **de** jardins* = a lot of gardens.
*Beaucoup **des** jardins* would mean *many **of the** gardens* — a specific, already-known set.
The second is grammatical but rare and means something different.

**la plupart** is the genuine exception: it always takes *des*. *La plupart des gens pensent
ça.*
:::

## Containers and measures

Same rule: the container, then **de**, then the substance.

::: examples
une tasse de café || a cup of coffee
un verre de vin || a glass of wine
une bouteille d'eau || a bottle of water
un kilo de pommes || a kilo of apples
une tranche de pain || a slice of bread
un morceau de gâteau || a piece of cake
:::

## Other quantity words

| Word | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **plusieurs** | several — no *de* | *plusieurs livres* |
| **quelques** | a few — no *de* | *quelques amis* |
| **certains / certaines** | some — no *de* | *certains jours* |
| **chaque** | each — always singular | *chaque jour*, *chaque personne* |
| **chacun / chacune** | each one — pronoun | *chacun a son avis* |
| **aucun / aucune** | none — always with *ne* | *je n'ai aucune idée* |
| **quelque chose** | something | *quelque chose de beau* |
| **tout le monde** | everyone — takes a **singular** verb | *tout le monde est là* |

::: warning plusieurs and quelques take no de
*plusieurs **de** livres* is wrong. It is simply *plusieurs livres*, *quelques amis*,
*certains jours*. Only the *beaucoup* family needs *de*.
:::

::: warning tout le monde is grammatically singular
*Tout le monde **est** là*, not *sont*. Likewise *chacun **a** son avis*. French agrees with
the grammatical form, not the real-world number.
:::
