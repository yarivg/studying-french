# Avoir, faire and the weather

English describes a state of the body with *to be*: I **am** hungry, I **am** cold, I **am**
twenty. French describes the same states with **avoir**, because it treats them as things you
have rather than things you are. The same instinct puts **faire** in charge of the weather and
of most activities, and the result is three lists worth learning whole.

# Avoir where English says *to be*

::: examples
J'ai faim. || I'm hungry.
J'ai soif. || I'm thirsty.
J'ai froid. || I'm cold.
J'ai chaud. || I'm hot.
J'ai sommeil. || I'm sleepy.
J'ai peur. || I'm afraid.
J'ai raison. || I'm right.
J'ai tort. || I'm wrong.
J'ai vingt ans. || I'm twenty.
:::

::: rule No article after avoir
*J'ai **faim***, not *j'ai la faim*. The noun sits bare, with no *le*, no *du*, no *un*. That
bareness is what turns the noun into something adjective-like, and it is what makes the rest of
the chapter behave the way it does.
:::

::: warning Je suis faim does not exist
*Je suis faim*, *je suis froid*, *je suis vingt ans*: none of these is French. *Je suis froid*
is grammatical but means *I am a cold person*, which is not what you wanted to say.

Age especially: **quel âge as-tu ?** and **j'ai vingt ans**. The *ans* is never dropped, unlike
English *I'm twenty*.
:::

## The intensifier

Because the noun is behaving like an adjective, it takes an adjective's intensifier.

::: examples
J'ai très faim. || I'm very hungry.
Il a très froid. || He's very cold.
J'ai si soif. || I'm so thirsty.
J'ai tellement sommeil. || I'm so sleepy.
:::

::: warning très with a noun, only here
*Très* normally cannot touch a noun, and *beaucoup* cannot touch an adjective. In these
expressions the rule is suspended: *j'ai **très** faim* is correct and *j'ai beaucoup faim* is
not. Treat it as a fixed exception, not as licence to write *très problème*.
:::

::: note bien, not très, with envie and besoin
*Envie* and *besoin* prefer **bien** or **vraiment**: *j'ai **bien** envie d'un café*, *j'ai
**vraiment** besoin de dormir*. *J'ai très envie* is heard, but *bien* is the safer default.
:::

# Avoir peur, besoin, envie, hâte: the ones with *de*

Four common expressions carry a **de** that never disappears. After it comes a noun or an
infinitive, and the choice of following article is the normal one.

::: examples
J'ai peur du noir. || I'm afraid of the dark.
J'ai peur de tomber. || I'm afraid of falling.
J'ai besoin d'aide. || I need help.
J'ai besoin d'un stylo. || I need a pen.
J'ai besoin de partir. || I need to leave.
J'ai envie d'un café. || I fancy a coffee.
J'ai envie de sortir. || I feel like going out.
J'ai hâte de te voir. || I can't wait to see you.
:::

::: rule de + le contracts, as always
*J'ai peur **du** chien*, *j'ai besoin **des** clés*, *j'ai envie **de la** soupe*. The *de*
belongs to the expression, but it still contracts with a following article the usual way.
:::

::: tip The infinitive is always bare after de
*J'ai besoin de **dormir***, *j'ai envie de **partir***, *j'ai hâte de **commencer***. There is
no conjugated verb and no *que* unless you go into the subjunctive, which is Part II.
:::

# Avoir mal à

Pain has its own frame: **avoir mal à** plus the body part, with *à* contracting the way it
always does.

| Body part | Form |
|---|---|
| le dos | *j'ai mal **au** dos* |
| le ventre | *j'ai mal **au** ventre* |
| la tête | *j'ai mal **à la** tête* |
| la gorge | *j'ai mal **à la** gorge* |
| l'estomac | *j'ai mal **à l'**estomac* |
| les dents | *j'ai mal **aux** dents* |
| les yeux | *j'ai mal **aux** yeux* |

::: warning The article is the definite one, not a possessive
French says *j'ai mal **à la** tête*, never *j'ai mal à ma tête*. The body part belongs to the
subject already, so the possessive is redundant, exactly as in *je me lave les mains*.
:::

::: note Related frames
*Ça fait mal.* - that hurts.
*Je me suis fait mal au genou.* - I hurt my knee.
*Il a mal partout.* - he aches all over.
:::

# Two more: l'air and la chance

::: examples
Il a l'air fatigué. || He looks tired.
Elle a l'air contente. || She looks happy.
Ça a l'air bon. || That looks good.
Tu as de la chance. || You're lucky.
Il n'a pas de chance. || He's unlucky.
:::

::: rule avoir l'air + adjective
*Avoir l'air* means *to look, to seem*, and takes an adjective, not an adverb. With a person,
agree the adjective with the subject: *elle a l'air **content**e*, *ils ont l'air **perdus***.
:::

::: warning avoir de la chance keeps its article
This is the one that breaks the bare-noun rule of the chapter: *chance* is a partitive, so it
is *j'ai **de la** chance*, and the negative flattens it to *je n'ai **pas de** chance*. Do not
write *j'ai chance*.
:::

# The weather: three patterns, not one

Learners mix these because English uses *it is* for all of them. French has three separate
frames, and a verb belongs to exactly one.

::: rule The three frames
**1. il fait + adjective** - the general feel of the day: *il fait beau*.
**2. il + weather verb** - one word does everything: *il pleut*.
**3. il y a + du / de la / des + noun** - a thing present in the sky: *il y a du vent*.
:::

::: examples
Il fait beau. || It's nice out.
Il fait mauvais. || The weather's bad.
Il fait chaud. || It's hot.
Il fait froid. || It's cold.
Il fait doux. || It's mild.
Il fait frais. || It's chilly.
Il fait gris. || It's grey out.
Il fait nuit. || It's dark.
Il fait jour. || It's light.
Il fait vingt degrés. || It's twenty degrees.
:::

::: examples
Il pleut. || It's raining.
Il neige. || It's snowing.
Il gèle. || It's freezing.
Il vente. || It's windy.
:::

::: examples
Il y a du vent. || It's windy.
Il y a du soleil. || It's sunny.
Il y a du brouillard. || It's foggy.
Il y a des nuages. || It's cloudy.
Il y a de l'orage. || There's a storm.
:::

::: warning il fait pleuvoir does not exist
*Pleuvoir*, *neiger* and *geler* are complete verbs and need no help: *il pleut*, *il neige*,
*il gèle*. *Il fait pleuvoir* would mean *he makes it rain*.

The reverse trap: *beau*, *chaud* and *froid* are adjectives and need *faire*. *Il beau* and
*il y a beau* are both wrong.
:::

::: note il fait du vent and il y a du vent both exist
Frame 1 and frame 3 overlap for the few weather words that are nouns: *il fait du vent*, *il
fait du soleil*, *il fait du brouillard* are all said, alongside the *il y a* versions. What
you cannot do is cross the frames: *il fait vent* and *il y a vent*, without the partitive, are
wrong either way.
:::

::: tip Asking about it
*Quel temps fait-il ?* - what's the weather like?
*Il fait quel temps ?* - the spoken version.
*Il fait combien ?* - what's the temperature?
:::

::: warning avoir chaud, faire chaud, être chaud
Three subjects, three verbs. *J'ai chaud* - I feel hot. *Il fait chaud* - the weather is hot.
*Le café est chaud* - the coffee is hot. A person never *est chaud* about temperature, and the
weather never *a chaud*.
:::

# Faire and jouer for activities

**Faire** covers sports, hobbies and housework. The article after it tells you which kind.

::: examples
Je fais du sport. || I do sport.
Je fais de la natation. || I swim / I go swimming.
Je fais du vélo. || I cycle.
Je fais de la randonnée. || I go hiking.
Je fais les courses. || I do the shopping.
Je fais la cuisine. || I do the cooking.
Je fais la vaisselle. || I do the washing-up.
Je fais le ménage. || I do the housework.
:::

::: rule Partitive for an activity, definite article for a chore
An activity you practise takes **du / de la**: *faire **du** sport*, *faire **de la**
natation*. A named household job takes **le / la / les**: *faire **la** cuisine*, *faire **la**
vaisselle*, *faire **les** courses*.
:::

## jouer à or jouer de

::: rule The one distinction to keep
**jouer à** + a game or a sport. **jouer de** + a musical instrument.
:::

::: examples
Je joue au foot. || I play football.
Elle joue au tennis. || She plays tennis.
On joue aux cartes. || We're playing cards.
Ils jouent aux échecs. || They're playing chess.
Je joue du piano. || I play the piano.
Elle joue de la guitare. || She plays the guitar.
Il joue du violon. || He plays the violin.
:::

::: warning Both prepositions contract
*à + le = au*, *à + les = aux*, *de + le = du*, *de + les = des*. So *jouer **au** foot* and
*jouer **aux** cartes*; *jouer **du** piano* and *jouer **de la** guitare*. Never *jouer à le
foot* or *jouer de le piano*.
:::

::: note faire de la musique or jouer de la musique
*Faire de la musique* means to be a musician, to play as a practice or a hobby. *Jouer de la
musique* means to perform it, here and now. The same pair works for an instrument: *je fais du
piano* is I learn and practise the piano, *je joue du piano* is I play it.
:::
