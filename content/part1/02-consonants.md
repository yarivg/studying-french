# Consonant sounds

Most French consonants are close enough to English to ignore. This chapter is about the ones
that are not: letters that change sound depending on what follows them, clusters that
simplify, and the **r** that everyone notices.

## The two letters that change: c and g

Both **c** and **g** are *hard* before **a, o, u** and *soft* before **e, i, y**. This is the
single most productive rule in French spelling.

| Letter | Before a, o, u | Before e, i, y |
|---|---|---|
| **c** | /k/ — *le café*, *contre*, *la culture*, *le carré* | /s/ — *le ciel*, *le citron*, *cinq*, *le cirque* |
| **g** | /g/ — *la gare*, *grave*, *le gant*, *le goût* | /ʒ/ — *le nuage*, *la gorge*, *gentil*, *la girafe* |

French then needs two tricks to break the rule when a word demands it:

| Trick | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **ç** (cédille) | forces /s/ before a, o, u | *le garçon*, *le reçu*, *ça*, *la façon*, *la leçon* |
| **-ge-** | keeps /ʒ/ before a, o | *nous mangeons*, *le nageoire*, *il mangeait* |
| **gu-** | forces hard /g/ before e, i | *la guitare*, *le guide*, *longue*, *la bague* |
| **qu-** | spells /k/ before e, i | *que*, *qui*, *quand*, *le cirque*, *chaque* |

::: warning gu and qu: the u is silent
In *la guitare*, *le guide*, *que*, *qui*, *quand*, *quart*, the **u** is not pronounced at
all. It is there purely to keep the g hard or spell the k. *La guitare* is /gitaʁ/, not
/gwitaʁ/, and *qui* is /ki/, not /kwi/.

The exceptions are rare and mostly technical: *aiguë*, *linguistique*, *équateur*.
:::

## Consonant combinations

| Written | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **ch** | /ʃ/ — English *sh* | *chanter*, *le chat*, *la chance*, *sèche*, *le chemin* |
| **ph** | /f/ | *la pharmacie*, *le téléphone*, *la photo* |
| **th** | /t/ — never English *th* | *le thé*, *le théâtre*, *Thomas* |
| **gn** | /ɲ/ — like the *ni* of *onion* | *le champignon*, *la montagne*, *l'agneau*, *la baignoire* |
| **ill** | /j/ | see the vowel chapter |
| **cc** before e, i | /ks/ | *l'accent*, *le succès*, *accepter* |
| **cc** elsewhere | /k/ | *l'accord*, *accrocher* |
| **sc** before e, i | /s/ | *descendre*, *l'ascenseur*, *la scène* |
| **sc** elsewhere | /sk/ | *le scandale*, *le score*, *l'escalier* |

## s between vowels becomes z

A single **s** with a vowel on each side is pronounced /z/. Doubled **ss** stays /s/.

| Spelling | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| vowel **s** vowel | /z/ | *la maison*, *la chaise*, *la valise*, *la saison*, *le vase* |
| **ss** | /s/ | *le poisson*, *la cuisson*, *aussi*, *la tasse* |

::: warning A pair worth getting right
*le poison* (poison) is /pwazɔ̃/ — one s, so /z/.
*le poisson* (fish) is /pwasɔ̃/ — two s, so /s/.

Same for *le désert* (/z/, the desert) and *le dessert* (/s/, pudding).
:::

## x

| Position | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **ex-** + vowel | /gz/ | *l'exemple*, *exister*, *exact*, *l'examen* |
| **ex-** + consonant | /ks/ | *le texte*, *exprimer*, *expliquer* |
| in *dix*, *six* alone | /s/ | *dix*, *six* |
| in *deuxième*, *dixième* | /z/ | *deuxième*, *sixième*, *dixième* |
| word-final, usually | silent | *le prix*, *la voix*, *le choix*, *heureux* |

## The French r

**r** is /ʁ/ — made at the back of the throat, roughly where you make a **g**, but with the
air continuing to flow. It is closer to the *ch* of Scottish *loch* than to any English r.

The most common mistake is not the sound itself but *inserting* an English r-colour into the
vowel before it. In *le père*, the vowel is a clean /ɛ/ followed by a separate /ʁ/ — the two
never blend.

::: tip Getting to /ʁ/
Say *aga, aga, aga*. Now soften the g so the air never fully stops. That is /ʁ/. Practise on
words where the r sits between vowels first — *la carrière*, *carré*, *la fureur* — before
attacking clusters like *quatre* or *le trottoir*.
:::

## Clusters that simplify

French drops letters inside certain clusters. These are worth memorising individually.

| Written | Said | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-mpt-** | /mt/ — the p vanishes | *compter*, *le comptable* |
| **-pt** final | /t/ | *sept* = /sɛt/ |
| **-mn-** | /n/ | *l'automne* = /otɔn/, *condamner* |
| **bs-** | /ps/ | *absolument*, *absent* |
| **-gt** | /t/ or silent | *longtemps* /lɔ̃tɑ̃/; *le doigt* /dwa/ (both g and t silent) |
| **-rf** | only the r | *le cerf*, *le nerf* |

## When the final consonant is heard

Most final consonants are silent (that is the next chapter). But **-ct** at the end of a word
is unpredictable, so learn these as pairs:

::: examples
exact, direct, strict || final **-ct** pronounced
le respect, le suspect, l'aspect || final **-ct** silent
:::

A useful check: if the word has a related form where the t is followed by a vowel, the t is
usually pronounced. *Direct* → *directement*, so /dirɛkt/.

## h is always silent — but there are two kinds

French never pronounces **h**. It still matters, because *h aspiré* blocks elision and
liaison as though a consonant were there.

| Type | Behaviour | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **h muet** | behaves like a vowel: elides and links | *l'homme*, *l'heure*, *l'hôpital*, *l'hiver* |
| **h aspiré** | behaves like a consonant: no elision, no liaison | *le hobby*, *le haut*, *la honte*, *le héros* |

::: warning There is no way to tell by looking
*L'homme* but *le hobby*. *L'heure* but *le haut*. The difference is etymological — *h aspiré*
words are mostly Germanic or English borrowings — and dictionaries mark them, often with an
asterisk or a superscript mark. In practice you learn them one at a time.
:::

An **h** between vowels also blocks the two vowels from running together: *dehors* is said
with a tiny break, /də.ɔʁ/, not as one smooth vowel.

## Doubled consonants

Doubled letters are not held longer than single ones, with one exception worth hearing:
doubled **nn** and **mm** keep the n or m audible and cancel any nasal.

::: examples
donner || /dɔne/ — the n is heard
l'année || /ane/ — compare *un an* /ɑ̃/, which is nasal
bonne || /bɔn/ — compare *bon* /bɔ̃/
la femme || /fam/ — irregular: the e is said /a/
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Hard or soft?
*le garçon* — why the cedilla? || Without it, *garcon* would be /gaʁkɔ̃/. The ç forces /s/ before **o**.
*la bague* — what is the u doing? || Keeping the g hard. Without it, *bage* would be /baʒ/.
*nous mangeons* — why the e? || It keeps /ʒ/ before **o**. *Mangons* would be /mɑ̃gɔ̃/.
*le cirque* — two c-like letters, two sounds? || The **c** before i is /s/; the **qu** is /k/. So /siʁk/.
:::

:::
