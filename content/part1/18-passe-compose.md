# Le passé composé

The passé composé is the everyday past tense — what you use to say what happened. It is built
from two pieces: an **auxiliary** in the present tense, and a **past participle**.

> **avoir** or **être** (present) + **past participle**

::: examples
J'ai mangé. || I ate / I have eaten.
Elle est partie. || She left.
Nous avons vu le film. || We saw the film.
:::

## Building the past participle

| Infinitive ending | Participle ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-er** | **-é** | *parler → parlé*, *manger → mangé* |
| **-ir** | **-i** | *finir → fini*, *choisir → choisi* |
| **-re** | **-u** | *vendre → vendu*, *attendre → attendu* |

All three endings sound different: /e/, /i/, /y/. That is the whole regular system.

::: tip Participles cluster by shape
Most irregulars end in **-u** (*vu, su, pu, bu, lu, vécu, connu, couru, voulu*), a smaller group
in **-is** (*mis, pris, appris, compris*), and a smaller one still in **-it / -ert**
(*dit, écrit, fait, ouvert, offert*). Learning them in these three piles is easier than one long
list.
:::

## avoir or être?

Most verbs take **avoir**. Two groups take **être**.

### Group 1: reflexive verbs — all of them

::: examples
Je me suis levé. || I got up.
Elle s'est habillée. || She got dressed.
Nous nous sommes dépêchés. || We hurried.
:::

### Group 2: a fixed list of intransitive verbs of movement and change of state

| Verb | Participle | | Verb | Participle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| aller | allé | | venir | venu |
| arriver | arrivé | | partir | parti |
| entrer | entré | | sortir | sorti |
| monter | monté | | descendre | descendu |
| naître | né | | mourir | mort |
| rester | resté | | tomber | tombé |
| retourner | retourné | | revenir | revenu |
| devenir | devenu | | rentrer | rentré |
| passer (by) | passé | | |

::: note DR & MRS VANDERTRAMP
The traditional mnemonic takes the first letter of each: **D**escendre, **R**ester,
**M**onter, **R**etourner, **S**ortir, **V**enir, **A**ller, **N**aître, **D**evenir,
**E**ntrer, **R**entrer, **T**omber, **R**evenir, **A**rriver, **M**ourir, **P**artir.

If mnemonics do not help you, the useful generalisation is: these verbs describe **going
somewhere or changing state**, and none of them can take a direct object.
:::

::: warning Six of them switch to avoir when they take an object
*monter*, *descendre*, *sortir*, *rentrer*, *retourner* and *passer* change auxiliary — and
meaning — depending on whether an object follows.

- *Je **suis** monté.* — I went up.
- *J'**ai** monté **les valises**.* — I took the suitcases up.
- *Elle **est** sortie.* — She went out.
- *Elle **a** sorti **son téléphone**.* — She took out her phone.
- *Nous **sommes** passés.* — We came by.
- *Nous **avons** passé **une semaine** à Paris.* — We spent a week in Paris.

The rule: **object present → avoir.**
:::

## Agreement

This is where most of the difficulty lives. There are three separate rules.

### With être: the participle agrees with the subject

Like an adjective: add **-e** for feminine, **-s** for plural.

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| Je (m.) | *je suis allé* |
| Je (f.) | *je suis allée* |
| Nous (m. or mixed) | *nous sommes allés* |
| Elles | *elles sont allées* |

::: examples
Je suis allé au marché. || I went to the market (m.)
Elle est venue tard. || She came late.
Nous sommes partis à huit heures. || We left at eight.
Vous êtes restés à la maison. || You stayed home.
Ils sont nés en France. || They were born in France.
:::

### With avoir: no agreement — unless the object comes first

The default is **no agreement at all**. *Elle a mangé*, not *mangée*.

But if a **direct object precedes the verb**, the participle agrees with it. This happens in
three situations:

::: examples
Les pommes que j'ai mangées. || relative pronoun *que* — object comes first
Je les ai mangées. || object pronoun — comes first
Quelles pommes as-tu mangées ? || question word — comes first
:::

Compare: *J'ai mangé **les pommes*** — object after the verb, so no agreement.

::: rule Ask one question: is the direct object before the verb?
- *J'ai mangé les pommes.* → object after → **mangé**
- *Je les ai mangées.* → object before → **mangées**

Nothing else matters. An indirect object never triggers agreement: *Je **leur** ai parlé*, not
*parlées*.
:::

### With reflexives: agree, unless a direct object follows

Reflexive verbs use *être*, so the instinct is to agree — and usually you do. But the real rule
is the *avoir* rule in disguise, because the reflexive pronoun is the object.

::: examples
Elle s'est lavée. || agreement — *se* is the direct object
Elle s'est lavé les mains. || no agreement — *les mains* is the direct object, *se* is indirect
Elles se sont parlé. || no agreement — *parler **à*** takes an indirect object
Elles se sont vues. || agreement — *voir* takes a direct object
:::

## Word order

| Element | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| negation (*pas, rien, jamais, plus*) | around the auxiliary | *Je **n'**ai **pas** mangé.* |
| negation (*personne, aucun, que*) | after the participle | *Je n'ai vu **personne**.* |
| object pronouns | before the auxiliary | *Je **l'**ai vu.* |
| short adverbs | between auxiliary and participle | *J'ai **bien** mangé.* · *J'ai **déjà** fini.* |
| long adverbs | after the participle | *J'ai répondu **immédiatement**.* |

::: examples
Je n'ai pas vu le film. ||
Je ne l'ai pas vu. ||
Nous ne nous sommes pas lavés. ||
J'ai déjà mangé. ||
Il n'a jamais compris. ||
:::
