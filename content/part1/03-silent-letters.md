# Silent letters and the final -e

French writes more than it says. The good news is that what gets dropped is highly regular:
final consonants, final **e**, and the plural markers. Once you internalise this, French
spelling stops looking arbitrary and starts looking like a system with the volume turned down
at the end of each word.

## Final consonants are silent

As a default, **the last consonant of a word is not pronounced**.

::: examples
le banc || /bɑ̃/ — no k sound
trop || /tʁo/
le bras || /bʁa/
le prix || /pʁi/
le nez || /ne/
le riz || /ʁi/
grand || /gʁɑ̃/
le sang || /sɑ̃/
:::

::: rule CaReFuL — the consonants that usually survive
The letters **c, r, f, l** at the end of a word are often *still pronounced*. The mnemonic is
the English word **CaReFuL**.

- **c**: *le parc*, *avec*, *sec*, *le lac*
- **r**: *le mur*, *pour*, *bonjour*, *sur*
- **f**: *neuf*, *le chef*, *l'œuf* (singular)
- **l**: *le sel*, *le ciel*, *seul*, *avril*

It is a tendency, not a law. *Le tabac*, *l'estomac*, *gentil*, *le fusil* and the whole
**-er** infinitive family (*parler*, *manger*) all break it.
:::

::: warning -er is /e/, not /ɛʁ/
Every **-er** infinitive ends in the sound /e/, exactly like **é**: *parler* = /paʁle/,
*manger* = /mɑ̃ʒe/, *travailler* = /tʁavaje/. The r is silent.

The same goes for *le boulanger*, *premier*, *léger*. But **-er** in a short word keeps its r:
*la mer*, *hier*, *l'hiver*, *le fer*, *cher*.
:::

## Final -e is silent

A final **e** with no accent is not pronounced. Its job is to make the consonant *before* it
audible.

| Without -e | With -e | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| *grand* /gʁɑ̃/ | *grande* /gʁɑ̃d/ | the **d** now sounds |
| *petit* /pəti/ | *petite* /pətit/ | the **t** now sounds |
| *vert* /vɛʁ/ | *verte* /vɛʁt/ | the **t** now sounds |
| *français* /fʁɑ̃sɛ/ | *française* /fʁɑ̃sɛz/ | the **s** now sounds, as /z/ |
| *heureux* /øʁø/ | *heureuse* /øʁøz/ | the **x** becomes /z/ |
| *long* /lɔ̃/ | *longue* /lɔ̃g/ | the **g** now sounds |

::: rule Why this matters more than it looks
This is the whole mechanism behind feminine adjectives and past participles. *Il est grand*
and *elle est grande* differ by exactly one audible consonant. If you swallow it, the
agreement disappears from your speech.
:::

The letter before the final **e** follows the normal soft/hard rules:

::: examples
la balade || the **d** is heard
la chance || **c** before e = /s/
manger || **g** before e = /ʒ/
la chaise || **s** between vowels = /z/
:::

## Vowel + silent e

When a vowel combination is followed by **e**, the vowel keeps its own sound and the e adds
nothing.

| Written | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-ie** | /i/ | *la bougie*, *la vie*, *la partie* |
| **-aie** | /ɛ/ | *la monnaie*, *la plaie*, *la craie* |
| **-ue** | /y/ | *la rue*, *bienvenue*, *la vue* |
| **-oie** | /wa/ | *la joie*, *la voie* |
| **-oue** | /u/ | *la roue*, *la boue* |
| **-eue** | /ø/ | *la queue* |
| **-ée** | /e/ | *l'année*, *la fée*, *l'épée*, *la durée* |

## The e in the middle of a word

An unaccented **e** inside a word is often dropped in ordinary speech. This is why French
sounds faster than it looks.

::: examples
le paiement || /pɛmɑ̃/ — three syllables written, two said
le remerciement || /ʁəmɛʁsimɑ̃/
maintenant || /mɛ̃tnɑ̃/ — the middle e disappears
la semaine || /səmɛn/ or /smɛn/
je ne sais pas || /ʃsɛpa/ in fast speech
:::

::: tip This is a spectrum, not a rule
Dropping these e's is normal, not sloppy. In careful or southern speech more of them survive;
in fast Parisian speech almost none do. You will be understood either way, but understanding
*others* requires expecting the dropped version.
:::

## Plurals are silent

The plural **-s** is never pronounced. Neither is the plural **-x**.

::: examples
le chien = les chiens || both /ʃjɛ̃/ — only the article changes
le prix = les prix || both /pʁi/
le gâteau = les gâteaux || both /gato/
:::

::: warning You hear the plural on the article, not the noun
Since the noun sounds identical, the *article* carries the whole distinction:
**le** /lə/ vs **les** /le/, **un** /œ̃/ vs **des** /de/. If you blur those, your listener
cannot tell singular from plural at all.
:::

## Verb endings are silent too

The **-ent** ending of the third person plural is completely silent. This makes three of the
six present-tense forms of an **-er** verb sound identical.

| Form | Written | Said |
|---|---|---|
| je | *chante* | /ʃɑ̃t/ |
| tu | *chantes* | /ʃɑ̃t/ |
| il / elle / on | *chante* | /ʃɑ̃t/ |
| nous | *chantons* | /ʃɑ̃tɔ̃/ |
| vous | *chantez* | /ʃɑ̃te/ |
| ils / elles | *chantent* | /ʃɑ̃t/ |

::: warning -ent on a verb is silent; -ent on other words is not
*ils chantent* → /ʃɑ̃t/, silent.
*content*, *le vent*, *lent*, *souvent* → nasal /ɑ̃/, definitely pronounced.

The silence is a property of the **verb ending**, not of the letters.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Pronounced or not?
*le banc* — is the **c** heard? || No. Final consonants are usually silent; this one is too.
*le parc* — is the **c** heard? || Yes. **c** is one of the CaReFuL consonants.
*parler* — is the **r** heard? || No. **-er** infinitives end in /e/.
*la mer* — is the **r** heard? || Yes. Short **-er** words keep it: *mer*, *hier*, *fer*, *cher*.
*grande* — is the **d** heard? || Yes. The silent final **e** exists to make it audible.
*ils parlent* — is the **-ent** heard? || No. Verb **-ent** is always silent.
*souvent* — is the **-ent** heard? || Yes — as a nasal /ɑ̃/. It is not a verb ending.
*les gâteaux* — is the **x** heard? || No. Plural markers are silent.
:::

::: exercise Masculine or feminine, by ear?
You hear /gʁɑ̃/. || *grand* — masculine. The feminine *grande* would end in /d/.
You hear /pətit/. || *petite* — feminine. The **t** is audible.
You hear /fʁɑ̃sɛz/. || *française* — feminine. The **s** sounds as /z/.
You hear /vɛʁ/. || *vert* — masculine.
You hear /øʁøz/. || *heureuse* — feminine.
:::
