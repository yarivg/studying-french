# L'impératif and le gérondif

Two small forms with outsized usefulness: one for telling people what to do, one for saying two
things happen at once.

# L'impératif

Used for orders, requests, advice, instructions, wishes and invitations. It exists in only
**three** persons — *tu*, *nous*, *vous* — and the pronoun itself disappears.

## Forming it

Take the present tense and delete the subject pronoun.

| Person | Present | Imperative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| tu | *tu finis* | **Finis !** | Finish! |
| nous | *nous finissons* | **Finissons !** | Let's finish! |
| vous | *vous finissez* | **Finissez !** | Finish! |

::: note There is no on imperative — but there is on
Spoken French says *let's* with **on** and a present tense far more often than with the *nous*
imperative: ***On y va !*** rather than *Allons-y !*, ***On mange ?*** rather than *Mangeons !*
Both are correct; *on* is what you will hear.
:::

::: rule -er verbs drop the final -s in the tu form
*tu manges* → **Mange !**
*tu vas* → **Va !**
*tu parles* → **Parle !**
*tu ouvres* → **Ouvre !**

This applies to every **-er** verb plus *aller*, *ouvrir*, *offrir* and *souffrir* — the ones
whose *tu* form ends in **-es**. Verbs in **-ir** and **-re** keep their s: *Finis !*,
*Attends !*, *Prends !*
:::

::: warning The s comes back before y and en
For pronunciation only: ***Vas**-y !* (go on), ***Manges**-en !* (have some). Without the s
there would be no liaison and the phrase would stumble.
:::

## The irregulars

Four verbs build their imperative from the subjunctive instead.

| Infinitive | tu | nous | vous |
|---|---|---|---|
| **avoir** | aie | ayons | ayez |
| **être** | sois | soyons | soyez |
| **savoir** | sache | sachons | sachez |
| **vouloir** | veuille | veuillons | **veuillez** |

::: examples
N'aie pas peur. || Don't be afraid.
Soyez heureux ! || Be happy!
Sachez que c'est important. || Be aware that it's important.
Veuillez vous asseoir. || Please have a seat.
:::

::: note veuillez is the politest word in French
*Veuillez* + infinitive is the standard formula for formal requests and signage:
*Veuillez patienter*, *Veuillez agréer mes salutations*. The alternative forms *veux / voulez*
exist but are rarely used as commands.
:::

## Pronouns with the imperative

| Command | Pronoun position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **positive** | after the verb, hyphenated | *Regarde-**le** !* · *Donne-**moi** ça !* |
| **negative** | before the verb, as normal | *Ne **le** regarde pas !* · *Ne **me** donne pas ça !* |

::: warning me becomes moi, te becomes toi — but only in a positive command
Positive: *Donne-**moi** le livre.* · *Lève-**toi** !* · *Tais-**toi** !*
Negative: *Ne **me** donne pas le livre.* · *Ne **te** lève pas !*
:::

Reflexive verbs follow the same pattern, which is why the pronoun ends up *after* the verb:

::: examples
Tais-toi ! || Shut up!
Réveille-toi ! || Wake up!
Réveillez-vous ! || Wake up! (formal / plural)
Levons-nous ! || Let's get up!
Amusez-vous bien ! || Enjoy yourselves!
Ne t'inquiète pas. || Don't worry.
:::

With two pronouns in a positive command, the order is **direct then indirect**:
*Donne-**le**-**moi**.* — give it to me.

## Everyday imperatives

::: examples
Cours ! || Run!
Viens ici. || Come here.
Écoute-moi. || Listen to me.
Tournez à gauche après le feu. || Turn left after the light.
N'oublie pas ! || Don't forget!
Fais attention. || Be careful.
Allons-y ! || Let's go!
Vas-y ! || Go on! / Go ahead!
Dépêche-toi ! || Hurry up!
:::

::: tip Softening a command
A bare imperative can sound blunt. French softens it with *s'il te plaît* / *s'il vous plaît*,
with the conditional (*Pourriez-vous… ?*), or by turning it into a question (*Tu peux fermer la
porte ?*). *Veuillez* is the formal option.
:::

# Le gérondif

**en** + the present participle. It expresses **simultaneity** — two actions at once — or
**manner** — the answer to *how?*

## Forming it

Take the **nous** form of the present tense, drop **-ons**, add **-ant**, and put **en** in
front.

| Infinitive | nous form | Gérondif |
|---|---|---|
| parler | nous **parl**ons | en parlant |
| finir | nous **finiss**ons | en finissant |
| vendre | nous **vend**ons | en vendant |
| faire | nous **fais**ons | en faisant |
| prendre | nous **pren**ons | en prenant |
| boire | nous **buv**ons | en buvant |
| lire | nous **lis**ons | en lisant |

Three irregulars, and they are the usual suspects:

| Infinitive | Gérondif |
|---|---|
| **avoir** | en **ayant** |
| **être** | en **étant** |
| **savoir** | en **sachant** |

## What it means

### Two actions at the same time

::: examples
Elles cuisinent en écoutant de la musique. || They cook while listening to music.
Il mange en regardant la télé. || He eats while watching TV.
Je lis en mangeant. || I read while eating.
:::

*En écoutant* is equivalent to *pendant qu'elles écoutent* — the gérondif is simply shorter.

### How something is done

::: examples
Il pratique la langue française en lisant des livres. || He practises French by reading books.
On apprend en faisant des erreurs. || You learn by making mistakes.
Elle est entrée en courant. || She came in running.
:::

::: rule One subject, always
The gérondif's action **must belong to the sentence's subject**.

*Elles cuisinent en écoutant de la musique* — *elles* both cook and listen. Correct.

If the two actions have different subjects, the gérondif is impossible and you need
*pendant que*: *Je cuisine **pendant qu'elle écoute** de la musique.*
:::

### tout en — insisting on the contrast

Adding **tout** stresses that the two things happen despite each other:

::: examples
Il travaille tout en écoutant de la musique. || He works, all the while listening to music.
Tout en étant fatigué, il a continué. || Although tired, he carried on.
:::
