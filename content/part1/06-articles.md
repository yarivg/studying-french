# Articles: le, un, du

French uses articles far more than English does. Where English drops them freely — *I like
coffee*, *cats are nice* — French almost never does. The question is not *whether* to use one
but *which*.

There are three families, and they answer three different questions.

| Family | Question it answers | Forms |
|---|---|---|
| **Definite** | which one? | le, la, l', les |
| **Indefinite** | one of several? | un, une, des |
| **Partitive** | how much of it? | du, de la, de l', des |

## Definite: le, la, les

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | le | les |
| **Feminine** | la | les |
| **Before a vowel or mute h** | l' | les |

Use it for a specific thing, *and* — unlike English — for general truths and abstractions.

::: examples
Le livre est sur la table. || The book is on the table. — a specific book
J'aime le chocolat. || I like chocolate. — chocolate in general
Les chiens sont fidèles. || Dogs are loyal. — dogs as a category
Le français est difficile. || French is difficult. — languages take an article
:::

::: warning English drops the article; French keeps it
*I like coffee* → **J'aime le café**, never *J'aime café*.
*Life is short* → **La vie est courte**.
*I study French* → **J'étudie le français**.

If a noun appears with no article at all in your French sentence, something is probably wrong.
:::

**à** and **de** contract with **le** and **les**:

| | + le | + les | (+ la, + l' unchanged) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **à** | **au** | **aux** | à la, à l' |
| **de** | **du** | **des** | de la, de l' |

::: examples
Je vais au marché. || à + le marché
Nous allons aux États-Unis. || à + les États-Unis
Le livre du professeur. || de + le professeur
La couleur des yeux. || de + les yeux
:::

## Indefinite: un, une, des

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | un | des |
| **Feminine** | une | des |

::: examples
J'ai un livre. || I have a book.
Elle a une idée. || She has an idea.
Il y a des enfants dans le jardin. || There are children in the garden.
:::

::: rule des has no English equivalent
English says *there are children*, with no word before the noun. French cannot: the plural
indefinite **des** is obligatory. *Il y a enfants* is not a sentence.
:::

## Partitive: du, de la, de l'

The partitive covers *some* of something uncountable — food, drink, materials, abstract
qualities. English usually says nothing at all, or *some*.

| | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | du | *du beurre*, *du pain*, *du courage* |
| **Feminine** | de la | *de la tarte*, *de la viande*, *de la patience* |
| **Before a vowel** | de l' | *de l'argent*, *de l'eau*, *de l'huile* |
| **Plural** | des | *des pâtes*, *des frites* |

::: examples
Je mange du pain. || I'm eating bread / some bread.
Elle boit de la bière. || She's drinking beer.
Il a de l'argent. || He has money.
Tu veux des frites ? || Do you want fries?
:::

::: warning Countable or not changes the article
- *Je mange **du** poulet.* — some chicken, an amount off a plate.
- *Je mange **un** poulet.* — a whole chicken. Different meal.
- *J'aime **le** poulet.* — chicken in general, as a preference.

All three are correct; they just say different things. The verb is a strong hint: *aimer*,
*adorer*, *détester*, *préférer* take the **definite** article, because you like the category,
not a portion.
:::

## The big rule: after a negative, everything becomes de

**un**, **une**, **du**, **de la** and **des** all collapse to **de** (or **d'**) after a
negative verb.

::: examples
J'ai un livre. → Je n'ai pas de livre. || I don't have a book.
J'ai une voiture. → Je n'ai pas de voiture. || I don't have a car.
J'ai une idée. → Je n'ai pas d'idée. || Elision before a vowel.
Je mange du pain. → Je ne mange pas de pain. || I don't eat bread.
Il y a des enfants. → Il n'y a pas d'enfants. || There aren't any children.
:::

::: warning Two exceptions
**1. The definite article never changes.**
*J'aime le café* → *Je n'aime pas **le** café*. Not *pas de café*.

**2. After être, the article survives.**
*C'est un livre* → *Ce n'est pas **un** livre*. Not *pas de livre*.
:::

## The other de rule: after a quantity

Every expression of quantity is followed by bare **de** — never *du*, *de la* or *des*, even
when the noun is plural.

| Expression | Example |
|---|---|
| beaucoup de | *beaucoup de jardins*, *beaucoup d'eau* |
| un peu de | *un peu de sucre* |
| trop de | *trop de bruit* |
| assez de | *assez de temps* |
| tant de | *tant de jardins* |
| autant de | *autant de travail* |
| plus / moins de | *plus de choix*, *moins de gens* |
| combien de | *combien de temps ?* |
| une tasse / un verre / un kilo de | *une tasse de café*, *un kilo de pommes* |

::: examples
Beaucoup de jardins. || A lot of gardens — not *beaucoup des jardins*.
Il y a beaucoup de gens ici. || There are a lot of people here.
J'ai trop de travail. || I have too much work.
Combien de temps ? || How long?
:::

::: note The one that keeps des
*bien des* means *many* and is the exception: *bien des gens pensent ça*. It is slightly
literary; *beaucoup de* is the everyday choice.
:::

## Choosing, in one table

| You mean | Article | Example |
|---|---|---|
| this specific one | le / la / les | *Passe-moi **le** sel.* |
| the category, in general | le / la / les | *J'aime **le** sel.* |
| one of many | un / une / des | *Il y a **un** problème.* |
| an unmeasured amount | du / de la / des | *Je veux **du** sel.* |
| a measured amount | quantity + de | *un peu **de** sel* |
| none, after a negative | de / d' | *Il n'y a pas **de** sel.* |
