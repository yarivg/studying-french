# Reflexive verbs

A reflexive verb carries an extra pronoun that refers back to the subject. French uses them
far more than English does: where English says *I get up*, French says *je **me** lève* — I
raise myself.

## The pronouns

| Subject | Reflexive pronoun |
|---|---|
| je | **me** (m') |
| tu | **te** (t') |
| il / elle / on | **se** (s') |
| nous | **nous** |
| vous | **vous** |
| ils / elles | **se** (s') |

The verb itself conjugates completely normally. The only new thing is the pronoun, which sits
**before** the verb.

| Pronoun | *se lever* | *se laver* | *s'habiller* |
|---|---|---|---|
| je | je me lève | je me lave | je m'habille |
| tu | tu te lèves | tu te laves | tu t'habilles |
| il / elle | il se lève | il se lave | il s'habille |
| nous | nous nous levons | nous nous lavons | nous nous habillons |
| vous | vous vous levez | vous vous lavez | vous vous habillez |
| ils / elles | ils se lèvent | ils se lavent | ils s'habillent |

::: note nous nous, vous vous
Yes, really. *Nous nous levons* looks like a typo and is not — the first *nous* is the subject,
the second is the reflexive pronoun.
:::

## Four things reflexives do

### 1. Genuinely reflexive — the action lands on yourself

::: examples
Je me lave. || I wash (myself).
Elle se regarde dans le miroir. || She looks at herself in the mirror.
Il se coupe. || He cuts himself.
:::

### 2. Reciprocal — to each other

Only in the plural, and often with *se … l'un l'autre* to remove ambiguity.

::: examples
Ils se parlent. || They talk to each other.
Nous nous voyons demain. || We're seeing each other tomorrow.
Elles se ressemblent. || They look alike.
Ils se manquent. || They miss each other.
:::

### 3. Idiomatic — the reflexive changes the meaning

This is the biggest group, and the pronoun no longer means *self* in any real sense.

| Plain verb | Reflexive | Meaning shift |
|---|---|---|
| *rendre* — to give back | **se rendre compte** | to realise |
| *passer* — to pass | **se passer** | to happen |
| *tromper* — to deceive | **se tromper** | to be mistaken |
| *entendre* — to hear | **s'entendre** | to get along |
| *demander* — to ask | **se demander** | to wonder |
| *attendre* — to wait | **s'attendre à** | to expect |
| *mettre* — to put | **se mettre à** | to start doing |
| *servir* — to serve | **se servir de** | to use |
| *plaindre* — to pity | **se plaindre** | to complain |
| *douter* — to doubt | **se douter** | to suspect |

::: examples
Qu'est-ce qui se passe ? || What's happening?
Je me suis trompé. || I made a mistake.
On s'entend bien. || We get along well.
Je me demande pourquoi. || I wonder why.
Il se sert de mon ordinateur. || He's using my computer.
:::

### 4. Instead of the passive

French dislikes the passive and often uses a reflexive instead.

::: examples
Ça se dit. || People say that / that's said.
Le vin blanc se boit frais. || White wine is drunk chilled.
Ça ne se fait pas. || That isn't done.
Comment ça s'écrit ? || How is that spelled?
:::

## Everyday reflexives worth knowing cold

| Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| se lever | to get up |
| se réveiller | to wake up |
| se coucher | to go to bed |
| se laver | to wash |
| s'habiller | to get dressed |
| se dépêcher | to hurry |
| se reposer | to rest |
| se promener | to go for a walk |
| s'amuser | to have fun |
| s'ennuyer | to be bored |
| se souvenir de | to remember |
| se rappeler | to remember |
| s'appeler | to be called |
| s'asseoir | to sit down |
| se taire | to be quiet |
| s'inquiéter | to worry |
| se sentir | to feel |
| se concentrer | to concentrate |
| se détendre | to relax |
| s'occuper de | to take care of |

::: warning se souvenir de vs se rappeler
Both mean *to remember*, but they take different structures:
*Je **me souviens de** cette journée.* — with **de**.
*Je **me rappelle** cette journée.* — with no preposition.

Mixing them (*je me rappelle de*) is extremely common among native speakers and still
considered incorrect in writing.
:::

## Where the pronoun goes

| Structure | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| present | before the verb | *Je **me** lève.* |
| negative | *ne* wraps the pronoun **and** the verb | *Je **ne me** lève **pas**.* |
| infinitive | the pronoun matches the subject | *Je vais **me** lever.* |
| positive command | after the verb; *te* → *toi* | *Lève-**toi** !* |
| negative command | back before the verb | *Ne **te** lève pas !* |
| passé composé | always with **être** | *Je **me suis** levé.* |

::: warning The infinitive pronoun still agrees
The reflexive pronoun always matches the **subject**, even when the verb is an infinitive:

*Je vais **me** lever.* · *Tu vas **te** lever.* · *Nous allons **nous** lever.*
*Nous aimons **nous** souvenir.* — we like to remember.

Writing *nous allons se lever* is wrong; the pronoun is not frozen as *se*.
:::

## Reflexives in the passé composé

Every reflexive verb takes **être**, with no exceptions. The participle usually agrees with the
subject.

::: examples
Je me suis levé. || I got up (m.)
Elle s'est levée. || She got up.
Nous nous sommes levés. || We got up.
Elles se sont levées. || They got up (f.)
Nous ne nous sommes pas lavés. || We didn't wash.
:::

::: warning No agreement when a direct object follows
The participle agrees only if the reflexive pronoun is the **direct** object. If a direct
object comes after the verb, the pronoun is indirect and nothing agrees:

*Elle s'est lavé**e**.* — she washed herself. Agreement.
*Elle s'est lavé **les mains**.* — she washed her hands. No agreement: *les mains* is the direct
object, and *se* is only indirect.

Same for *elle s'est cass**é** la jambe* — she broke her leg.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Conjugate
je (se lever) || je me lève
nous (se laver) || nous nous lavons
il (s'habiller) || il s'habille
vous (se dépêcher) || vous vous dépêchez
elles (se réveiller) || elles se réveillent
tu (s'appeler) || tu t'appelles
:::

::: exercise Put it in the right place
(se lever) Je ___ tôt. || Je me lève tôt.
(se lever) Je ne ___ pas tôt. || Je ne me lève pas tôt.
(se lever) Je vais ___ tôt. || Je vais me lever tôt.
(se lever) ___ ! (command, tu) || Lève-toi !
(se lever) Ne ___ pas ! (command, tu) || Ne te lève pas !
(se lever) Nous allons ___ tôt. || Nous allons nous lever tôt. || not *se lever*
:::

::: exercise Agreement in the passé composé
Elle (se laver). || Elle s'est lavée.
Elle (se laver) les mains. || Elle s'est lavé les mains. || no agreement — *les mains* is the direct object
Nous (se lever). || Nous nous sommes levés.
Elles (se parler). || Elles se sont parlé. || *parler à* — indirect, so no agreement
Il (se casser) la jambe. || Il s'est cassé la jambe.
:::
