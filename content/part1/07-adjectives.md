# Adjectives

A French adjective agrees with its noun in gender and number. That is four possible forms for
every adjective, and the endings are almost entirely predictable.

## The default pattern

Add **-e** for feminine, **-s** for plural, **-es** for both.

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **Singular** | grand | grande |
| **Plural** | grands | grandes |

::: examples
un grand jardin || a big garden
une grande maison || a big house
de grands jardins || big gardens
de grandes maisons || big houses
:::

Remember from the pronunciation chapter: the feminine **-e** makes the previous consonant
audible. *Grand* is /gʁɑ̃/, *grande* is /gʁɑ̃d/. The agreement is something you *hear*.

## When the base form already ends in -e or -s

| Situation | Result | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| already ends in **-e** | no change in the feminine | *jeune*, *libre*, *rouge*, *facile*, *sage*, *timide* |
| already ends in **-s** or **-x** | no change in the masculine plural | *gris → gris*, *heureux → heureux*, *vieux → vieux* |

::: examples
un homme jeune / une femme jeune || *jeune* is already -e
un jour gris / des jours gris || masculine plural unchanged
un homme heureux / des hommes heureux || same
:::

## Irregular feminines

These patterns cover almost everything you will meet.

| Masculine ending | Feminine | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-eux** | **-euse** | *heureux → heureuse*, *paresseux → paresseuse*, *délicieux → délicieuse* |
| **-eur** | **-euse** | *menteur → menteuse*, *trompeur → trompeuse* |
| **-teur** | **-trice** | *créateur → créatrice*, *protecteur → protectrice* |
| **-f** | **-ve** | *neuf → neuve*, *actif → active*, *vif → vive* |
| **-er** | **-ère** | *cher → chère*, *premier → première*, *fier → fière*, *léger → légère* |
| **-en** | **-enne** | *italien → italienne*, *ancien → ancienne* |
| **-on** | **-onne** | *bon → bonne*, *mignon → mignonne* |
| **-el** | **-elle** | *cruel → cruelle*, *naturel → naturelle* |
| **-et** | **-ète** or **-ette** | *complet → complète*; *muet → muette* |
| **-c** | **-che** or **-que** | *blanc → blanche*, *sec → sèche*; *public → publique* |
| **-s** | **-sse** (some) | *gros → grosse*, *bas → basse*, *gras → grasse* |

Fully irregular, and worth memorising outright:

| Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|
| beau | belle |
| nouveau | nouvelle |
| vieux | vieille |
| fou | folle |
| doux | douce |
| faux | fausse |
| long | longue |
| frais | fraîche |
| roux | rousse |

## The five with a third form

**Beau**, **nouveau**, **vieux**, **fou** and **mou** have a special masculine form used before
a vowel or mute h. It exists purely so the phrase is pronounceable.

| Masculine | Before a vowel | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| beau | **bel** | belle |
| nouveau | **nouvel** | nouvelle |
| vieux | **vieil** | vieille |
| fou | **fol** | folle |
| mou | **mol** | molle |

### How the middle column sounds

One rule covers all five: **the before-a-vowel form is said exactly like the feminine.**
*bel* and *belle* are the same sound, *vieil* and *vieille* are the same sound. The spelling
loses the doubled letter and the final *-e*, but nothing about the pronunciation changes.

| Form | Sounds like | IPA | Say it as | The trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bel | belle | /bɛl/ | **bèl** | not "bell": the l is light, tongue forward against the teeth |
| nouvel | nouvelle | /nuvɛl/ | **nou-vèl** | the first syllable is the rounded ou, never "know" |
| vieil | vieille | /vjɛj/ | **vyèy** | the final -il is a **y** sound, not an l at all |
| fol | folle | /fɔl/ | **fol** | one short open o, the vowel of "sort" |
| mol | molle | /mɔl/ | **mol** | same shape as fol, and rare outside set phrases |

Then that final consonant does not sit at the end of the word: it slides onto the vowel it
was invented for, so the two words come out as one. *un bel homme* is said **un bè-lomme**,
and *un nouvel ami* is **un nou-vè-lami**. *vieil* passes its y sound across the same way.

::: examples
un beau garçon || the normal masculine, no linking
un bel homme || *bè-lomme*, one block of sound
un nouvel ami || *nou-vè-lami*, not *un nouveau ami*
un vieil arbre || *vyè-yarbre*, not *un vieux arbre*
un vieil homme || the h is mute, so it links just like a vowel
un fol espoir || *fo-lespoir*, a wild hope
:::

::: rule Why this form exists at all
Say *un beau homme* aloud: two vowels collide with nothing to join them, and French dislikes
that. *bel* supplies a consonant to bridge the gap. That is also why the feminine never needs
a third form: *belle*, *nouvelle*, *vieille* already end in a consonant sound, so *une belle
amie* links without help.
:::

Press the speaker on any line above to hear it. Shift-click reads it slowly, which is the
setting worth using for *vieil arbre* until the y sound is automatic.

## Position: after the noun, usually

Unlike English, the French adjective normally follows its noun.

::: examples
une voiture rouge || a red car
un livre intéressant || an interesting book
la langue française || the French language
un homme fatigué || a tired man
:::

Colours, shapes, nationalities, religions and past participles are always after the noun.

## The ones that go before: BAGS

A small set of very common adjectives precedes the noun. The usual mnemonic is **BAGS** —
**B**eauty, **A**ge, **G**oodness, **S**ize.

| Group | Adjectives |
|---|---|
| Beauty | *beau*, *joli* |
| Age | *jeune*, *vieux*, *nouveau*, *ancien* |
| Goodness | *bon*, *mauvais*, *meilleur*, *gentil* |
| Size | *grand*, *petit*, *gros*, *long*, *haut* |

Plus a few outside the mnemonic: *autre*, *même*, *tout*, *chaque*, *premier*, *dernier*.

::: examples
une jolie fille || a pretty girl
un bon repas || a good meal
un petit problème || a small problem
la première fois || the first time
:::

::: rule des becomes de before a preceding adjective
When a plural adjective comes *before* the noun, **des** shortens to **de**:

*des jardins* but ***de** beaux jardins*
*des idées* but ***de** bonnes idées*

In casual speech many people keep *des*. In writing, use *de*.
:::

## Adjectives that change meaning with position

This is the reason position is worth caring about. Before the noun, the adjective is
figurative; after it, literal.

| Adjective | Before the noun | After the noun |
|---|---|---|
| **ancien** | *un ancien professeur* — a former teacher | *un livre ancien* — an antique book |
| **cher** | *un cher ami* — a dear friend | *un livre cher* — an expensive book |
| **grand** | *un grand homme* — a great man | *un homme grand* — a tall man |
| **pauvre** | *un pauvre homme* — a pitiable man | *un homme pauvre* — a man with no money |
| **propre** | *ma propre chambre* — my own room | *une chambre propre* — a clean room |
| **dernier** | *la dernière semaine* — the final week | *la semaine dernière* — last week |
| **seul** | *un seul homme* — a single man | *un homme seul* — a lonely man |
| **certain** | *un certain âge* — a certain age | *un fait certain* — an undoubted fact |

## Adjectives that shift nuance, not meaning

*heureux* is not in BAGS, yet it can still go in front of the noun. Nothing flips the way
*ancien* flips: both positions mean something close to *happy*. What moves is what the
adjective is describing.

| Position | What it describes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Before | the event or the outcome: *fortunate, lucky* | *un heureux hasard* (a lucky coincidence) · *un heureux événement* (a happy event, i.e. a baby) · *un heureux choix* (a fortunate choice) |
| After | the person and their mood: *happy* | *un homme heureux* (a happy man) · *une famille heureuse* · *un visage heureux* |

::: rule Which one do you want?
Ask whether you are describing **how someone feels** or **how something turned out**.

*Il est heureux.* He is happy. A mood, so it can only follow.
*C'est un heureux hasard.* It turned out well. A judgement on the event, so it precedes.
:::

A handful of others behave the same way: a preceding position turns the adjective into a
judgement, and it usually sounds a shade more literary.

| Adjective | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| **heureux** | *un heureux hasard*: fortunate | *un homme heureux*: happy |
| **curieux** | *un curieux personnage*: an odd character | *un enfant curieux*: an inquisitive child |
| **triste** | *un triste individu*: a wretched individual | *un homme triste*: a sad man |
| **vrai** | *un vrai problème*: a real problem (emphasis) | *une histoire vraie*: a true story |

::: warning Do not generalise this
Most adjectives cannot move. *un rouge livre* and *une française langue* are simply wrong.
Fronting is licensed word by word: BAGS, the meaning-changing list above, and this short
nuance list. Everything else stays after the noun.
:::

## Adverbs of degree: beaucoup vs très

Both translate loosely as *very / a lot*, but they attach to different things.

| Word | Modifies | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **très** | an adjective or an adverb | *Elle est très intelligente.* · *Il court très vite.* |
| **beaucoup** | a verb, or a quantity with *de* | *J'aime beaucoup le chocolat.* · *beaucoup de gens* |

::: warning Never *très beaucoup*, never *beaucoup intelligent*
- *I like it a lot* → **J'aime beaucoup**, not *j'aime très*.
- *very intelligent* → **très intelligent**, not *beaucoup intelligent*.
- *very much* → **beaucoup** on its own. *Merci beaucoup.*

A useful test: if English would accept *very*, use **très**. If it would accept *a lot*, use
**beaucoup**.
:::

Other degree words: *assez* (quite, enough), *trop* (too), *un peu* (a little), *plutôt*
(rather), *vraiment* (really), *tellement* (so).
