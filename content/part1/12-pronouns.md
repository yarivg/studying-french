# Pronouns

French has four sets of personal pronouns, and which one you need depends entirely on the
grammatical job the word is doing. Get the job right and the form follows.

## The four columns

| Person | Subject | Direct object | Indirect object | Stressed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | **je** | **me** (m') | **me** (m') | **moi** |
| you (informal) | **tu** | **te** (t') | **te** (t') | **toi** |
| he / she | **il / elle** | **le / la** (l') | **lui** | **lui / elle** |
| we | **nous** | **nous** | **nous** | **nous** |
| you (formal / plural) | **vous** | **vous** | **vous** | **vous** |
| they | **ils / elles** | **les** | **leur** | **eux / elles** |
| we / people (spoken) | **on** | none — uses **nous** | none — uses **nous** | **soi** |

Four of the six rows are identical across three columns, so the real work is in the *il/elle*
and *ils/elles* rows. **On** is the seventh person you will hear more than any other: it is a
subject only, it takes the *il* verb form, and where it needs an object it borrows *nous*.

## Subject pronouns

::: examples
Je parle français. || I speak French.
Tu es fatigué ? || Are you tired?
Elle habite à Paris. || She lives in Paris.
Nous allons au marché. || We're going to the market.
:::

::: note on — the pronoun that does everything
**On** takes a third-person singular verb — the same form as *il* — but means, depending on
context:

- **we** — in speech this has almost entirely replaced *nous*: *On y va !* (Let's go)
- **one / people in general** — *On ne fume pas ici.* (No smoking here)
- **someone** — *On m'a dit que…* (Someone told me / I was told)

*On mange à huit heures* is what you will actually hear; *nous mangeons* sounds careful.

Conjugate it exactly like *il*, in every tense: *on **est***, *on **a** mangé*, *on **allait***,
*on **ira***, *qu'on **soit***.
:::

::: rule Everything about on that is not the verb
| Point | How it works |
|---|---|
| object pronoun | there is none; use *nous* — *On nous a vus.* |
| reflexive | **se** — *On **se** lève à sept heures.* |
| possessive | **son / sa / ses** — *On fait **son** travail.* |
| stressed form | **soi** — *On reste chez **soi**.* |
| adjective / participle | agrees with the real meaning when *on* = we — *On est **contents***, *on est **allés*** |
| after *si*, *que*, *où*, *et* | often written **l'on**, for sound only — *si **l'on** veut* |

The verb stays singular even when the agreement after it is plural: *On est allé**s*** has a
singular *est* and a plural *allés*. That combination looks wrong and is correct.
:::

::: warning tu or vous?
**tu** for one friend, family member, child, colleague you are close to, or anyone your own
age in an informal setting. **vous** for one stranger, anyone older, any professional
situation — and always for more than one person.

Getting it wrong in the *vous* direction is merely distant. Getting it wrong in the *tu*
direction can be rude. When unsure, use *vous*; the other person will invite you to switch
with *on peut se tutoyer*.
:::

## Direct object pronouns

The direct object is what the verb acts on with no preposition between them: *I see **the
book*** → *je **le** vois*.

::: examples
Tu vois le livre ? — Oui, je le vois. || I see it.
Tu connais Marie ? — Oui, je la connais. || I know her.
Il achète les billets. → Il les achète. || He buys them.
Elle m'aime. || She loves me.
Je vous comprends. || I understand you.
:::

**The pronoun goes before the verb**, not after it. This is the biggest structural difference
from English.

::: warning Word order: before the verb, always
English: *I see **him***. French: *Je **le** vois* — literally *I him see*.

With an infinitive, the pronoun attaches to the infinitive:
*Je vais **le** voir.* — I'm going to see him.
*Je veux **les** acheter.* — I want to buy them.
:::

## Indirect object pronouns

The indirect object is the person **to whom** something is done. In French, that means the verb
uses **à** before a person.

::: examples
Je parle à Marie. → Je lui parle. || I speak to her.
Il donne le livre à Paul. → Il lui donne le livre. || He gives him the book.
Nous téléphonons à nos amis. → Nous leur téléphonons. || We phone them.
Elle m'a répondu. || She answered me.
:::

::: rule The test is the preposition, not the English
Ask: does this verb take **à** before a person? If yes, use **lui / leur**. If no, use
**le / la / les**.

The trap is that English and French disagree about which verbs those are:
- *téléphoner **à** quelqu'un* → *je **lui** téléphone* (English has no *to*)
- *écouter quelqu'un* → *je **l'**écoute* (English says *listen **to***)
- *regarder quelqu'un* → *je **le** regarde* (English says *look **at***)
- *attendre quelqu'un* → *je **l'**attends* (English says *wait **for***)
:::

Verbs that reliably take **à** + person, and therefore **lui / leur**:

*parler à*, *dire à*, *donner à*, *demander à*, *répondre à*, *téléphoner à*, *écrire à*,
*envoyer à*, *offrir à*, *montrer à*, *expliquer à*, *plaire à*, *ressembler à*, *obéir à*.

## Stressed pronouns

Used wherever a pronoun stands **outside** the verb's immediate grip.

| Situation | Example |
|---|---|
| after a preposition | *avec **moi***, *sans **lui***, *chez **eux***, *pour **toi*** |
| after *c'est* | *C'est **moi**.* · *C'est **eux**.* |
| standing alone | *Qui veut du café ? — **Moi** !* |
| for emphasis | ***Moi**, je pense que non.* |
| in a comparison | *plus grand que **lui*** |
| with *aussi* / *non plus* | ***Moi** aussi.* · ***Moi** non plus.* |
| joined by *et* | ***Toi** et **moi**.* |

::: examples
Viens avec moi. || Come with me.
C'est pour toi. || It's for you.
Moi, je préfère le thé. || Me, I prefer tea.
Je suis plus grand que lui. || I'm taller than him.
Chez eux, on dîne à vingt heures. || At their place, dinner is at eight.
:::

::: warning moi aussi vs moi non plus
Answering a **positive** statement: **moi aussi** (me too).
Answering a **negative** statement: **moi non plus** (me neither).

*— Je suis fatigué. — **Moi aussi**.*
*— Je ne suis pas fatigué. — **Moi non plus**.*

Using *moi aussi* after a negative is a very common learner error.
:::

## Where the pronoun sits

| Structure | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| simple tense | before the verb | *Je **le** vois.* |
| negative | between *ne* and the verb | *Je ne **le** vois pas.* |
| compound tense | before the auxiliary | *Je **l'**ai vu.* |
| with an infinitive | before the infinitive | *Je vais **le** voir.* |
| positive command | **after** the verb, hyphenated | *Regarde-**le** !* |
| negative command | back to normal position | *Ne **le** regarde pas !* |

::: warning Commands flip the order — and me becomes moi
Positive: *Donne-**moi** le livre.* · *Écoute-**moi** !* · *Lève-**toi** !*
Negative: *Ne **me** donne pas le livre.* · *Ne **te** lève pas !*

In a positive command only, **me** → **moi** and **te** → **toi**.
:::
