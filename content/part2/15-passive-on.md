# The passive, and on

French has a passive voice and mostly avoids it. Where English reaches for *was built*, *is
said*, *has been decided*, French usually prefers **on** or a reflexive. Knowing the passive is
useful for reading; knowing the alternatives is what makes you sound French.

## Forming the passive

> **être** (in any tense) + **past participle**, agreeing with the subject

| Tense | Example |
|---|---|
| present | *La maison **est construite**.* |
| passé composé | *La maison **a été construite**.* |
| imperfect | *La maison **était construite**.* |
| future | *La maison **sera construite**.* |
| conditional | *La maison **serait construite**.* |

::: rule The participle always agrees
Because *être* is the auxiliary, the participle behaves like an adjective:
*La maison est construit**e***, *les maisons sont construit**es***.
:::

## Naming the agent

Use **par** for an action, **de** for a state or feeling.

::: examples
La maison a été construite par mon grand-père. || an action — par
Le voleur a été arrêté par la police. || an action — par
Il est respecté de tous. || a feeling — de
Elle est aimée de ses collègues. || a feeling — de
:::

Most of the time the agent is simply omitted: *La maison a été construite en 1920.*

## Why French avoids it

The passive is heavy in French. Three lighter alternatives cover almost every case.

### 1. on + active verb

**On** is the workhorse. Whenever English has a passive with no named agent, French usually uses
*on* with an active verb.

::: examples
On parle français ici. || French is spoken here.
On m'a dit que… || I was told that…
On a construit cette maison en 1920. || This house was built in 1920.
On a volé ma voiture. || My car has been stolen.
Ici, on ne fume pas. || No smoking here.
:::

::: rule on takes a third-person singular verb
*On **est** là*, *on **va** partir*, *on **a** mangé*. Always singular, even when *on* clearly
means *we*.

The participle may agree with the real-world meaning in careful writing — *on est
allé**s*** when *on* means *nous* — but the verb stays singular.
:::

### 2. A reflexive verb

::: examples
Ça se dit. || That's said / people say that.
Ça ne se fait pas. || That isn't done.
Le vin blanc se boit frais. || White wine is drunk chilled.
Comment ça s'écrit ? || How is that spelled?
Ce livre se vend bien. || This book sells well.
Le français se parle dans 29 pays. || French is spoken in 29 countries.
:::

This is the natural choice when the subject is a thing and no agent is relevant.

### 3. se faire + infinitive

For something that happened **to** someone, often unwelcome:

::: examples
Il s'est fait voler son portefeuille. || He got his wallet stolen.
Elle s'est fait renverser par une voiture. || She was knocked down by a car.
Je me suis fait avoir. || I got had / I was tricked.
Il s'est fait virer. || He got fired.
:::

::: warning The participle of se faire never agrees
*Elle s'est **fait** renverser*, not *faite*. Followed by an infinitive, *fait* is invariable.
:::

## The three uses of on

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| **we** — the everyday spoken form | *On y va ?* — Shall we go? |
| **people in general** | *En France, on dîne tard.* |
| **someone / they** | *On m'a volé mon sac.* — Someone stole my bag. |

::: rule In speech, on has replaced nous
*On mange à huit heures* is what people say. *Nous mangeons à huit heures* is what people write.

Using *nous* constantly in conversation is not wrong, but it is noticeably formal — a little
like saying *one* in English.
:::

::: examples
On va au cinéma ce soir ? || Shall we go to the cinema?
Nous, on préfère rester. || Us, we'd rather stay. — *nous* as emphasis, *on* as subject
On dit que c'est difficile. || They say it's difficult.
:::

::: note l'on in formal writing
After *si*, *et*, *ou*, *où* and *que*, formal French sometimes writes **l'on** instead of *on*
purely for sound: *si **l'on** veut*, *où **l'on** va*. It means nothing extra.
:::

## Choosing between them

| Situation | Best choice | Example |
|---|---|---|
| the agent matters | passive with *par* | *Le livre a été écrit par Camus.* |
| formal or written register | passive | *La décision a été prise.* |
| no agent, everyday speech | **on** | *On a pris la décision.* |
| a general truth about a thing | reflexive | *Ça se fait comme ça.* |
| something bad happened to someone | *se faire* | *Il s'est fait voler.* |

::: warning An indirect object cannot become a subject
English can say *I was told*, *he was given a book*. French cannot — its passive only promotes a
**direct** object.

*On m'a dit.* — I was told.
*On lui a donné un livre.* — He was given a book.

There is no *je suis dit* or *il est donné un livre*. For these, **on** is not merely preferred;
it is the only option.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Turn it into the passive
La police a arrêté le voleur. || Le voleur a été arrêté par la police.
Mon grand-père a construit cette maison. || Cette maison a été construite par mon grand-père.
On prendra la décision demain. || La décision sera prise demain.
Tout le monde le respecte. || Il est respecté de tout le monde.
:::

::: exercise Rewrite with on
French is spoken here. || On parle français ici.
I was told that he's leaving. || On m'a dit qu'il part.
My car has been stolen. || On a volé ma voiture.
This house was built in 1920. || On a construit cette maison en 1920.
He was given a book. || On lui a donné un livre.
:::

::: exercise Which alternative fits best?
*How is that spelled?* || Comment ça s'écrit ? — reflexive
*He got his wallet stolen.* || Il s'est fait voler son portefeuille. — *se faire*
*The book was written by Camus.* || Le livre a été écrit par Camus. — passive; the agent matters
*That isn't done.* || Ça ne se fait pas. — reflexive
*Shall we go?* || On y va ? — *on*
:::

::: exercise What does on mean here?
*On y va ?* || We — shall we go?
*En France, on dîne tard.* || People in general.
*On m'a volé mon sac.* || Someone — my bag was stolen.
*Nous, on préfère rester.* || We — with *nous* added for emphasis.
:::

::: exercise Translate
No smoking here. || Ici, on ne fume pas.
White wine is drunk chilled. || Le vin blanc se boit frais.
I got tricked. || Je me suis fait avoir.
This book sells well. || Ce livre se vend bien.
The decision has been made. || La décision a été prise. / On a pris la décision.
:::
