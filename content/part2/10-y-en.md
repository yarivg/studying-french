# Y, en and pronoun order

Two tiny words that replace whole phrases. They are everywhere in spoken French, and leaving
them out is one of the clearest signs of a learner.

# Y

**Y** replaces **à + a thing or a place**. Never a person.

## Y = there

::: examples
Je vais à Paris. → J'y vais. || I'm going there.
Il habite en France. → Il y habite. || He lives there.
Tu es au bureau ? — Oui, j'y suis. || Yes, I'm there.
:::

It covers any preposition of place: *à*, *en*, *dans*, *sur*, *sous*, *chez*.

::: examples
Les clés sont sur la table. → Elles y sont. ||
Je vais chez le médecin. → J'y vais. ||
:::

## Y = à + a thing

With verbs that take **à** before a thing, *y* replaces the whole phrase.

::: examples
Je pense à mon travail. → J'y pense. || I'm thinking about it.
Il répond à la question. → Il y répond. || He answers it.
Tu joues au tennis ? — Oui, j'y joue. || Yes, I play it.
Je m'intéresse à la musique. → Je m'y intéresse. ||
:::

::: warning à + a person is lui / leur, not y
*Je pense **à mon frère*** → *Je pense **à lui***, not *j'y pense*.
*Je parle **à Marie*** → *Je **lui** parle*.

**y** is for things and places only. For people after *à*, use an indirect object pronoun — or,
with verbs like *penser à* and *s'intéresser à*, a stressed pronoun after the verb.
:::

## Fixed expressions

::: examples
Il y a || there is / there are
Ça y est ! || That's it! / Done!
Allons-y ! || Let's go!
Vas-y ! || Go on!
Je m'y connais. || I know about it.
On y va ? || Shall we go?
:::

# En

**En** replaces **de + something**, and anything introduced by a quantity.

## En = de + a thing

::: examples
Je parle de mon travail. → J'en parle. || I'm talking about it.
J'ai besoin d'aide. → J'en ai besoin. || I need it.
Il revient de Paris. → Il en revient. || He's coming back from there.
Je me souviens de ce jour. → Je m'en souviens. || I remember it.
:::

## En = some / any

This is the use with no English equivalent, and the one most often forgotten.

::: examples
Tu veux du café ? — Oui, j'en veux. || Yes, I'd like some.
Tu as des frères ? — Non, je n'en ai pas. || No, I don't have any.
Il achète du pain. → Il en achète. || He buys some.
:::

::: rule English can drop "some"; French cannot drop en
*Do you want coffee? — Yes, I do.*
*Tu veux du café ? — Oui, j'**en** veux.*

Answering *oui, je veux* is not French. Whenever the noun disappears but a quantity is implied,
**en** has to appear.
:::

## En with numbers and quantities

The number or quantity **stays**, at the end.

::: examples
J'ai deux frères. → J'en ai deux. || I have two (of them).
Il y a beaucoup de gens. → Il y en a beaucoup. || There are a lot.
Je veux un kilo de pommes. → J'en veux un kilo. ||
Tu as combien de livres ? — J'en ai dix. ||
:::

::: warning un and une come back at the end
*J'ai **un** frère* → *J'**en** ai **un***, not *j'en ai*.
*Elle a **une** voiture* → *Elle **en** a **une***.

The article survives as a number. This is the *phrase elliptique* pattern: **subject + en + verb
+ amount**.

*J'en veux un.* — I want one of them.
*J'en prends une tranche.* — I'll take a slice of it.
*Peux-tu m'en donner un ?* — Can you give me one?
:::

# Pronoun order

When several pronouns pile up before the verb, they follow a fixed queue.

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| me / te / se / nous / vous | le / la / les | lui / leur | **y** | **en** |

::: examples
Il me le donne. || He gives it to me. — 1 then 2
Je le lui donne. || I give it to him. — 2 then 3
Il y en a. || There is some. — 4 then 5
Je t'en parle. || I'll tell you about it. — 1 then 5
Elle nous les a montrés. || She showed them to us.
Je le leur ai dit. || I told them so.
:::

::: tip A way to hold the order
The first column is the person-ish pronouns (*me, te, se, nous, vous*), then the thing (*le, la,
les*), then the recipient (*lui, leur*), then place (*y*), then quantity (*en*).

**y** and **en** are always last, and in that order. *Il y en a* is the phrase to anchor it.
:::

## In a positive command, the order changes

After a positive imperative, the pronouns go **after** the verb and the direct object comes
first.

::: examples
Donne-le-moi. || Give it to me.
Donne-m'en. || Give me some.
Allons-y. || Let's go.
Parle-lui-en. || Talk to him about it.
:::

Negative commands revert to the normal order: *Ne me le donne pas.*
