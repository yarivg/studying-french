# Impersonal verbs

English says *it is raining* without anyone asking what *it* is. French does the same with *il*,
and does it far more often: *il faut*, *il reste*, *il suffit*, *il semble*, *il y a*. The
reflex to fix is that *il* means *he*. In *il faut partir* there is no *he* anywhere in the
sentence; the *il* is a placeholder, put there because a French verb refuses to stand without
a subject.

::: rule Third person singular, always
An impersonal verb has one form and never agrees with anything. There is no *elle faut*, no
*ils restent deux places*, no *nous falloir*. Whatever follows the verb, the verb stays
singular.
:::

::: examples
Il pleut. || It's raining.
Il fait froid. || It's cold.
Il est huit heures. || It's eight o'clock.
Il y a du monde. || There are a lot of people.
:::

::: note Weather and time are the easy ones
*Il pleut*, *il fait beau*, *il est midi*: you have been using impersonal verbs since Part I
without calling them that. The rest of the chapter applies the same mechanism to necessity,
quantity and likelihood.
:::

# Il faut

The most used verb in the language after the auxiliaries, and the one worth learning in full.
It means *it is necessary*, and English translates it a dozen ways: *must*, *have to*, *need*,
*should*, *one must*. *Falloir* exists only in the impersonal: no *je faux*, no *nous fallons*.

## Il faut + infinitive

The general statement, true for anyone. No named subject, so no *que*.

::: examples
Il faut partir. || We have to go.
Il ne faut pas fumer ici. || You mustn't smoke here.
Il faut être patient. || One has to be patient.
:::

::: warning il ne faut pas is a prohibition
*Il ne faut pas fumer* means you **must not** smoke, not that you need not. For *you don't
have to*, French changes verb: *tu n'es pas obligé de fumer*, *ce n'est pas la peine de*.
:::

## Il faut que + subjonctif

When the obligation lands on a named person, *que* appears, and after *que* comes the
subjunctive. This is the single commonest subjunctive trigger in the language: see Part II
chapter 8.

::: examples
Il faut que je parte. || I have to go.
Il faut que tu fasses attention. || You need to be careful.
:::

::: rule Infinitive or que: who is doing it?
No person named: **infinitive**, *il faut manger*. A specific person: **que + subjonctif**,
*il faut que tu manges*. Same test as *vouloir*: *je veux partir* against *je veux que tu
partes*.
:::

## Il faut + noun

With a noun instead of a verb, *il faut* means *it takes* or *it needs*. Add an indirect
pronoun to say who needs it.

::: examples
Il faut du courage. || It takes courage.
Il faut deux heures pour y aller. || It takes two hours to get there.
Il me faut du temps. || I need time.
:::

::: warning The pronoun is indirect, and it goes before faut
*Il **me** faut du temps*, never *il faut à moi*. The person is *me, te, lui, nous, vous,
leur*, sitting in front of the verb like any indirect object.
:::

## The other tenses

Only *falloir* changes; the *il* never does.

| Tense | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | il faut | *Il faut partir.* |
| **Imperfect** | il fallait | *Il fallait le dire !* - you should have said so |
| **Passé composé** | il a fallu | *Il a fallu deux heures.* - it took two hours |
| **Future** | il faudra | *Il faudra attendre.* - we'll have to wait |
| **Conditional** | il faudrait | *Il faudrait partir.* - we ought to go |
| **Subjunctive** | qu'il faille | *Je doute qu'il faille payer.* |

::: tip il faudrait is the polite one
*Il faut partir* is an order. *Il faudrait partir* softens it to *we ought to go*, which is
what you say to someone you are not in charge of.
:::

# Il vaut mieux

*It is better to*. Built on *valoir*, frozen in the third person, and it takes the same two
shapes as *il faut*: infinitive in general, *que* + subjunctive for a named person.

::: examples
Il vaut mieux attendre. || It's better to wait.
Il vaut mieux ne rien dire. || Better to say nothing.
Il vaut mieux que tu partes. || You'd better go.
:::

::: warning Not il vaut mieux de
No preposition before the infinitive: *il vaut mieux **attendre***, not *il vaut mieux
d'attendre*. Same as *il faut attendre*.
:::

# Il s'agit de

Treated in chapter 18, and it belongs here: its subject can only ever be the impersonal *il*.
*Il s'agit d'un roman policier*, *de quoi s'agit-il ?*, *il ne s'agit pas de ça*.

# The rest of the family

## Il reste, il manque

Both take a quantity, and both keep the verb singular no matter how big the quantity is.

::: examples
Il reste du pain. || There's some bread left.
Il reste deux places. || There are two seats left.
Il manque une chaise. || There's a chair missing.
Il manque trois pages. || Three pages are missing.
:::

::: rule The real subject sits after the verb
In *il reste deux places*, the grammatical subject is *il* and *deux places* is the logical
subject, parked after the verb. The verb answers to *il*, so it stays singular: **il reste**
deux places, never *ils restent deux places*.
:::

## Il suffit de, il arrive que

::: examples
Il suffit de demander. || You only have to ask.
Il arrive que je me trompe. || I sometimes get it wrong.
Il arrive qu'il pleuve en juin. || It does rain in June sometimes.
:::

::: note il arrive takes the subjunctive
*Il arrive que* reports something occasional rather than asserting it, so the verb after *que*
goes to the subjunctive: *il arrive qu'il **pleuve***. Compare the plain *il arrive*,
*he arrives*, not impersonal at all.
:::

# Indicatif or subjonctif

Four of the family look interchangeable in English and are not. The line French draws is
between what you assert and what you only entertain.

| Expression | Mood | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **il paraît que** | indicatif | *Il paraît qu'il **est** malade.* |
| **il est probable que** | indicatif | *Il est probable qu'il **viendra**.* |
| **il semble que** | subjonctif | *Il semble qu'il **soit** malade.* |
| **il est possible que** | subjonctif | *Il est possible qu'il **vienne**.* |

::: rule Certainty takes the indicative, doubt takes the subjunctive
*Il paraît que* passes a report on as fact and *il est probable que* is confident enough to
count as an assertion, so both take the indicative. *Il semble que* backs away from the claim
and *il est possible que* asserts nothing at all, so both take the subjunctive. The full list
of triggers is in Part II chapter 8.
:::

::: warning il me semble que goes back to the indicative
Name the person doing the seeming and the hedge becomes a statement about you, which you do
assert: *il me semble qu'il **a** raison*, against *il semble qu'il **ait** raison*.
:::

# Il y a

The oldest member of the family, covered in full in Part I chapter 22: *il y a un problème*,
*il y a dix livres*, one form for both because nothing agrees with the dummy *il*.

# The mistakes

::: warning Ce livre s'agit de
A book cannot *s'agir*. Say *ce livre **parle** de la guerre*, or *dans ce livre, **il
s'agit** de la guerre*. The subject of *s'agir* is always *il*.
:::

::: warning Il faut que je pars
*Que* brings the subjunctive: *il faut que je **parte***. If the form will not come, drop the
*que* and say *je dois partir*.
:::

::: warning Ils restent deux places
The verb answers to the dummy *il*, not to the quantity behind it: *il reste deux places*,
*il manque trois pages*, *il y a dix livres*.
:::

::: warning Il est un problème
*Il est* is not a general-purpose *it is*. Before a noun with an article, French wants *c'est*:
*c'est un problème*, *c'est mon frère*. *Il est* stays for the impersonal frames, *il est huit
heures*, *il est possible que*, and for a real *he*. Part III chapter 7 sorts the pair out.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Infinitive or subjonctif?
Il faut ___ (partir) tout de suite. || partir
Il faut que tu ___ (partir) tout de suite. || partes
Il vaut mieux ___ (attendre). || attendre
Il suffit de ___ (demander). || demander
Il faut que nous ___ (faire) attention. || fassions
:::

::: exercise Indicatif or subjonctif?
Il paraît qu'il ___ (être) malade. || est
Il semble qu'il ___ (être) malade. || soit
Il est probable qu'il ___ (venir). || viendra / vient
Il est possible qu'il ___ (venir). || vienne
Il arrive qu'il ___ (pleuvoir) en juin. || pleuve
:::

::: exercise Put il faut in the tense asked for
___ le dire ! (imperfect) || Il fallait
___ deux heures. (passé composé) || Il a fallu
___ recommencer. (future) || Il faudra
___ partir maintenant. (conditional) || Il faudrait
:::

::: exercise Fix the mistake
Ce livre s'agit de la guerre. || Dans ce livre, il s'agit de la guerre. / Ce livre parle de la guerre.
Il faut que je pars. || Il faut que je parte. - que brings the subjunctive
Ils restent deux places. || Il reste deux places. - the verb stays singular
Il faut à moi du temps. || Il me faut du temps. - the pronoun is indirect and comes first
Il vaut mieux d'attendre. || Il vaut mieux attendre. - no preposition
Il est un problème. || C'est un problème. - c'est before a noun with an article
:::
