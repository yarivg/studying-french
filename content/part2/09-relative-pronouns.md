# Relative pronouns

A relative pronoun joins two sentences by standing in for a noun already mentioned. Which one
you need depends entirely on the **grammatical hole** it fills in the second sentence — not on
whether the noun is a person or a thing.

::: rule The test
Build the second sentence on its own and see what is missing.

- missing the **subject** → **qui**
- missing the **direct object** → **que**
- missing **de** + something → **dont**
- missing a **place or time** → **où**
:::

## qui — the subject

The verb that follows has no other subject, so *qui* is it.

::: examples
L'homme **qui** parle est mon frère. || The man who is speaking… — *qui* is the subject of *parle*.
Le livre **qui** est sur la table. || The book that is on the table.
Les gens **qui** arrivent en retard attendront. || The people who arrive late…
:::

**Qui** works for people and things alike, and it **never elides**: *l'homme **qui a**…*, never
*qu'a*.

## que — the direct object

The verb that follows already has its own subject; *que* is what the verb acts on.

::: examples
L'homme **que** j'aime s'appelle Boris. || The man whom I love — *j'* is the subject, *que* the object.
Le livre **que** tu lis. || The book that you're reading.
La ville **que** nous avons visitée. || The city we visited.
:::

**Que** elides to **qu'** before a vowel: *l'homme **qu'**il aime*.

::: warning The clearest test: what follows?
- **qui** is followed by a **verb**: *l'homme qui **parle***
- **que** is followed by a **subject**: *l'homme que **je** connais*

If a new subject appears right after the pronoun, you need *que*. This test is fast and almost
never wrong.
:::

::: warning English can drop it; French cannot
*The book I'm reading* — English omits *that*. French must keep **que**:
*Le livre **que** je lis.* Never *le livre je lis*.
:::

### que triggers participle agreement

Because *que* puts a direct object **before** the verb, the past participle agrees with it.

::: examples
La ville que nous avons visitée. || *visitée* — agrees with *la ville*, feminine
Les livres que j'ai lus. || *lus* — agrees with *les livres*, masculine plural
Les pommes que j'ai mangées. || *mangées*
:::

## où — place and time

::: examples
La ville **où** j'habite. || The city where I live.
Le pays **où** je suis né. || The country where I was born.
Le jour **où** je l'ai rencontré. || The day (when) I met him.
Le moment **où** tout a changé. || The moment when everything changed.
:::

::: warning où covers "when", not quand
For a relative clause about time, French uses **où**, not *quand*:
*le jour **où** je suis arrivé* — the day when I arrived.
*Le jour quand…* is wrong. *Quand* asks questions and introduces subordinate clauses, but it
does not act as a relative pronoun.
:::

## dont — replacing de + something

**Dont** stands in for any phrase that would use **de**. It covers English *whose*, *of which*,
*about which* — and, crucially, whatever preposition the French verb happens to take.

### 1. With verbs that take de

::: examples
Le film **dont** je parle. || The film I'm talking about. — *parler **de** quelque chose*
La personne **dont** j'ai besoin. || The person I need. — *avoir besoin **de***
Le problème **dont** il s'agit. || The problem in question. — *il s'agit **de***
Ce **dont** j'ai peur. || What I'm afraid of. — *avoir peur **de***
:::

### 2. Meaning *whose*

::: examples
L'homme **dont** la voiture est rouge. || The man whose car is red.
La femme **dont** le fils travaille ici. || The woman whose son works here.
Un livre **dont** la couverture est déchirée. || A book whose cover is torn.
:::

::: warning After dont, French uses le/la, not a possessive
English: *the man whose **car**…* French: *l'homme dont **la** voiture…*

Never *dont sa voiture*. The article does the work; *dont* has already supplied the
possession.
:::

### 3. Meaning *of which*, in quantities

::: examples
Il a trois sœurs, dont deux habitent à Paris. || …two of whom live in Paris.
J'ai lu dix livres, dont trois en français. || …three of them in French.
:::

::: rule Learn dont through the verb, not the English
You cannot predict *dont* from English, because English uses different prepositions. Ask
instead: **does this French verb take de?**

*parler de*, *avoir besoin de*, *avoir peur de*, *se souvenir de*, *rêver de*, *s'occuper de*,
*être fier de*, *dépendre de*, *se servir de*, *il s'agit de* — all of these give **dont**.
:::

## lequel — after other prepositions

When the preposition is anything other than *de*, use **lequel** and its forms. They agree with
the noun.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | lequel | lesquels |
| Feminine | laquelle | lesquelles |

With **à**, they contract: *auquel*, *à laquelle*, *auxquels*, *auxquelles*.

::: examples
La table **sur laquelle** j'ai posé mes clés. || The table on which I put my keys.
Le projet **auquel** je travaille. || The project I'm working on.
Les raisons **pour lesquelles** il est parti. || The reasons for which he left.
L'entreprise **dans laquelle** elle travaille. || The company she works for.
:::

::: warning For people, prefer qui after a preposition
*La personne **avec qui** je travaille* is more natural than *avec laquelle*.
*L'ami **à qui** j'ai parlé.*

**Lequel** is the choice for things; **qui** for people. Both are correct, but this is the
usual split.
:::

## ce qui, ce que, ce dont

When there is no noun to attach to — the antecedent is a whole idea, or unknown — add **ce**.

| Form | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ce qui** | subject | *Je ne sais pas **ce qui** se passe.* |
| **ce que** | object | *Je ne sais pas **ce que** tu veux.* |
| **ce dont** | with *de* | *Je ne sais pas **ce dont** tu parles.* |

::: examples
Ce qui m'énerve, c'est le bruit. || What annoys me is the noise.
Dis-moi ce que tu veux. || Tell me what you want.
C'est ce dont j'ai besoin. || That's what I need.
Il est parti, ce qui m'a surpris. || He left, which surprised me.
:::

::: rule Tout ce qui / tout ce que
*Everything that* is **tout ce qui** or **tout ce que**, following the same subject/object split:

*Tout **ce qui** brille n'est pas or.* — all that glitters (subject).
*Tout **ce que** je sais.* — everything I know (object).
:::
