# Near future and recent past

Three constructions that place an action just before or just after now. All three are built the
same way — a conjugated verb plus an infinitive — and all three are used constantly in speech.

## Le futur proche: aller + infinitive

> **aller** (present) + **infinitive**

| Pronoun | Form |
|---|---|
| je | vais partir |
| tu | vas partir |
| il / elle | va partir |
| nous | allons partir |
| vous | allez partir |
| ils / elles | vont partir |

::: examples
Je vais partir. || I'm going to leave.
Il va pleuvoir. || It's going to rain.
Nous allons manger. || We're about to eat.
Tu vas tomber ! || You're going to fall!
:::

This is the **default** future in conversation. If you are speaking rather than writing, and the
event is not remote, use this.

### Negation and pronouns

The negation wraps *aller*; the pronoun attaches to the infinitive.

::: examples
Je ne vais pas partir. || ne … pas around the conjugated verb
Je vais le voir. || pronoun before the infinitive
Je ne vais pas le voir. || both at once
Je vais me lever. || reflexive pronoun matches the subject
:::

::: warning aller + infinitive vs aller = to go
*Je vais **partir*** — I'm going to leave (future).
*Je vais **à Paris*** — I'm going to Paris (movement).

An infinitive after *aller* makes it a future auxiliary; a place after it keeps it a verb of
movement. *Je vais manger* is *I'm going to eat*, not *I'm going in order to eat* — for that,
French would need *je vais pour manger* or a different verb entirely.
:::

## Le passé récent: venir de + infinitive

> **venir** (present) + **de** + **infinitive**

| Pronoun | Form |
|---|---|
| je | viens de partir |
| tu | viens de partir |
| il / elle | vient de partir |
| nous | venons de partir |
| vous | venez de partir |
| ils / elles | viennent de partir |

::: examples
Je viens de manger. || I've just eaten.
Il vient d'arriver. || He's just arrived.
Nous venons de finir. || We've just finished.
Elle vient de partir. || She's just left.
:::

::: rule venir de is present tense but means the past
*Je **viens** de manger* uses a present-tense verb to describe something already finished. Do
not be tempted into *je suis venu de manger* — that is not French.

The **imperfect** version shifts the whole thing one step back: *je **venais** de manger quand
il est arrivé* — I had just eaten when he arrived.
:::

::: warning venir de + infinitive vs venir de + place
*Je viens de **manger*** — I've just eaten.
*Je viens de **Paris*** — I come from Paris.

Again the infinitive is the signal.
:::

## Être en train de + infinitive

> **être** (present) + **en train de** + **infinitive**

This is French's answer to the English continuous, used only when you need to insist that
something is happening *right now*.

::: examples
Je suis en train de manger. || I'm in the middle of eating.
Ne le dérange pas, il est en train de travailler. || Don't disturb him, he's working right now.
J'étais en train de dormir quand tu as appelé. || I was in the middle of sleeping when you called.
:::

::: warning Do not use it as a default present
The ordinary present already covers *I am eating*. *Je mange* is the normal way to say it.
**Être en train de** adds emphasis on the in-progress-ness, and using it for every English
continuous makes your French heavy.

Use it when the point *is* the interruption or the simultaneity.
:::

## The three on one timeline

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *venir de* + inf. | just happened | *Je viens de manger.* |
| *être en train de* + inf. | happening now | *Je suis en train de manger.* |
| *aller* + inf. | about to happen | *Je vais manger.* |

Each shifts back one step if you put its conjugated verb in the imperfect:

| Imperfect version | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Je venais de manger.* | I had just eaten. |
| *J'étais en train de manger.* | I was in the middle of eating. |
| *J'allais manger.* | I was going to eat. |

::: examples
J'allais partir quand il est arrivé. || I was about to leave when he arrived.
Je venais de partir quand il a téléphoné. || I had just left when he phoned.
:::

## Futur proche or futur simple?

| | Futur proche | Futur simple |
|---|---|---|
| Register | spoken | written and spoken |
| Distance | near, planned | any, often distant |
| Certainty | feels certain | neutral |

::: examples
Attention, tu vas tomber ! || Only the futur proche works — it's imminent.
En 2050, les villes seront différentes. || Only the futur simple — remote and abstract.
Je vais te rappeler. / Je te rappellerai. || Both fine; the first is what you'd say.
:::

::: tip The safe default
In speech, use **aller + infinitive** unless the event is genuinely remote. In writing, prefer
the **futur simple**. Neither is ever grammatically wrong, so this is a question of sounding
natural, not of being correct.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Build the form
je (aller + partir) || je vais partir
nous (aller + manger) || nous allons manger
il (venir de + arriver) || il vient d'arriver
elles (venir de + finir) || elles viennent de finir
je (être en train de + travailler) || je suis en train de travailler
je (aller + se lever) || je vais me lever
je (aller + partir, negative) || je ne vais pas partir
:::

::: exercise Which construction?
He's just arrived. || Il vient d'arriver.
He's about to arrive. || Il va arriver.
He's arriving right now. || Il est en train d'arriver.
He had just arrived. || Il venait d'arriver.
He was about to arrive. || Il allait arriver.
:::

::: exercise Fix or approve
*Je suis venu de manger.* || Wrong. *Je viens de manger* — *venir de* stays in the present.
*Je vais à manger.* || Wrong. No preposition: *je vais manger*.
*Je viens de Paris.* || Correct — but it means "I come from Paris", not "I've just Paris".
*J'allais partir quand il est arrivé.* || Correct — imperfect *aller* = "was about to".
:::

I'm going to call you. || Je vais t'appeler.
I've just called him. || Je viens de l'appeler.
I'm in the middle of calling her. || Je suis en train de l'appeler.
I'm not going to go. || Je ne vais pas y aller.
You're going to fall! || Tu vas tomber !
:::
