# Verbs that keep their pronoun

Chapter 10 treated *en* and *y* as replacements: something was said, and the pronoun stands in
for it. *J'ai besoin d'aide* → *j'en ai besoin*, where *en* is *d'aide* and you could put the
words back.

A second family behaves differently. The verb has swallowed the pronoun for good, and nothing
can be put back. *Je m'en vais* means *I'm leaving*, and there is no answer to "*en* what?".
These are not constructions you can work out. They are words, and they are learned whole.

::: rule The test: can you say what the pronoun replaces?
*J'en veux deux.* — *en* = *de ces pommes*. Recoverable. This is Chapter 10.
*Je m'en vais.* — *en* = nothing. Frozen. This is Chapter 18.

If the pronoun has no antecedent anywhere in the conversation and the sentence is still
correct, you are looking at a fixed expression. Learn it as one block, pronoun included.
:::

# S'en aller

The most common of them, and the model for the rest. It means *to leave, to go away*, and it
carries both a reflexive pronoun and *en*.

| | |
|---|---|
| je | je m'en vais |
| tu | tu t'en vas |
| il / elle / on | il s'en va |
| nous | nous nous en allons |
| vous | vous vous en allez |
| ils / elles | ils s'en vont |

The reflexive pronoun changes with the subject; *en* never moves.

::: examples
Je m'en vais, il est tard. || I'm off, it's late.
Elle s'en est allée sans rien dire. || She left without saying a thing.
Ne t'en va pas ! || Don't go!
Va-t'en ! || Go away!
Allez-vous-en. || Leave. (formal / plural)
Je vais m'en aller. || I'm going to leave.
:::

::: warning Three places the pieces move
**Compound tense** — *être*, and the participle agrees: *elle s'en est allée*, *ils s'en sont
allés*. The pronouns stay in front of the auxiliary, never around the participle.

**Positive command** — the pronouns go behind, and *te* becomes *t'* before *en*:
*va-**t'en***, *allons-nous-**en***, *allez-vous-**en***.

**Negative command** — everything reverts to the normal order: *ne **t'en** va pas*.
:::

::: note s'en aller or partir?
They overlap. *Partir* is the neutral verb and takes a destination: *je pars à Lyon*.
*S'en aller* is about the leaving itself, is more spoken, and rarely takes a destination:
*bon, je m'en vais*. You cannot say *je m'en vais à Lyon* comfortably.
:::

# The en family

::: examples
en vouloir à quelqu'un || to hold it against someone
s'en faire || to worry
s'en sortir || to manage, to pull through
en avoir marre / en avoir assez || to be fed up
ne plus en pouvoir || to be at the end of one's rope
en avoir pour || to need (a length of time)
s'en prendre à quelqu'un || to take it out on someone
ne pas en revenir || to not get over it
en finir avec || to be done with
en valoir la peine || to be worth it
s'en tenir à || to stick to
en venir à || to reach the point of
:::

::: examples
Il m'en veut depuis des mois. || He's been holding it against me for months.
Ne m'en veux pas. || Don't hold it against me.
Ne t'en fais pas, ça ira. || Don't worry, it'll be fine.
Il s'en sort très bien. || He's managing very well.
J'en ai marre de ce bruit. || I'm fed up with this noise.
Je n'en peux plus. || I can't take it any more.
J'en ai pour cinq minutes. || I'll be five minutes.
Ne t'en prends pas à moi. || Don't take it out on me.
Je n'en reviens pas. || I can't believe it.
Il faut en finir avec cette histoire. || We have to put an end to this business.
Ça en vaut la peine. || It's worth it.
Tenons-nous-en à ce qui est décidé. || Let's stick to what was decided.
J'en viens à me demander s'il ment. || I'm starting to wonder whether he's lying.
:::

::: warning en vouloir à takes an indirect object
The person is *à* + someone, so a pronoun is **lui / leur**, not a stressed pronoun:
*Il **m'en** veut.* *Je **lui en** veux.* *Ils **nous en** veulent.*

Never *il en veut à moi*. And note the queue: the person pronoun comes first, *en* last,
exactly as in Chapter 10.
:::

# The y family

::: examples
ça y est || that's it, done
y aller || to go, to get going
s'y prendre || to go about something
s'y connaître (en) || to know one's way around
y tenir || to care about it
s'y faire || to get used to it
n'y être pour rien || to have nothing to do with it
y être || to get it, to follow
:::

::: examples
Ça y est, j'ai fini. || That's it, I'm done.
On y va ? || Shall we go?
Tu t'y prends mal. || You're going about it the wrong way.
Il s'y connaît en vin. || He knows his wine.
J'y tiens beaucoup. || It means a lot to me.
Tu t'y feras. || You'll get used to it.
Je n'y suis pour rien. || It's nothing to do with me.
Ah, j'y suis ! || Ah, I've got it!
:::

# Il s'agit de

A fixed expression with no personal subject at all. It means *it is about*, *it is a question
of*, and the subject can only ever be *il*.

::: examples
Il s'agit d'un roman policier. || It's a detective novel.
De quoi s'agit-il ? || What is it about?
Il s'agissait de partir avant midi. || It was a matter of leaving before noon.
Il ne s'agit pas de ça. || That's not the point.
:::

::: warning The mistake everyone makes once
A book cannot *s'agir* of anything. *Ce livre s'agit de la guerre* is not French.

Say *Ce livre **parle** de la guerre*, or *Dans ce livre, **il s'agit** de la guerre*.
The subject of *s'agir* is always the impersonal *il*, and there is no *je m'agis*,
no *nous nous agissons*.
:::

# Je t'en prie

Worth its own section because it is the one you will say daily. Literally *prier quelqu'un
**de** quelque chose* — to beg someone for something — with the *de* frozen into *en*.

| | |
|---|---|
| informal | **Je t'en prie.** |
| formal or plural | **Je vous en prie.** |

Three uses, and tone decides which:

::: examples
Merci beaucoup ! — Je vous en prie. || You're welcome.
Je peux m'asseoir ? — Je vous en prie. || Please do, be my guest.
Je t'en prie, arrête. || I'm begging you, stop.
:::

::: tip You're welcome, from casual to formal
*De rien.* — the everyday one, what you will say most of the time
*Pas de quoi.* — very casual
*Il n'y a pas de quoi.* — the full version of the same
*Je t'en prie.* — polite, to someone you *tu*
*Je vous en prie.* — polite, to someone you *vous*
*Avec plaisir.* — warm, my pleasure
:::
