# Imparfait vs passé composé

This is the hardest choice in French grammar for English speakers, because English marks the
distinction only sometimes and French marks it always. The good news: it becomes mechanical
once you stop translating and start asking one question.

::: rule The question to ask
**Does this move the story forward, or set the scene?**

- Moves it forward → **passé composé**
- Sets the scene → **imparfait**

Everything below is a refinement of that single test.
:::

## The core contrast

| Passé composé | Imparfait |
|---|---|
| an event | a state |
| completed, bounded | ongoing, unbounded |
| happened once (or a countable number of times) | happened repeatedly, habitually |
| moves the narrative | describes the background |
| *what happened* | *what was going on* |

::: examples
Hier, j'ai mangé une pizza. || An event. It happened, it finished.
Quand j'étais petit, je mangeais des pizzas. || A habit. No single occasion.
Il a plu hier. || It rained — a bounded event.
Il pleuvait quand je suis sorti. || It was raining — the background to my leaving.
:::

## The classic pattern: background + interruption

Most past narration mixes the two in the same sentence. The imperfect sets the stage; the passé
composé is what breaks in.

::: examples
Je **lisais** quand le téléphone **a sonné**. || I was reading when the phone rang.
Il **pleuvait** quand nous **sommes partis**. || It was raining when we left.
Elle **dormait** quand je **suis rentré**. || She was asleep when I got home.
:::

::: tip Quand tells you nothing on its own
Both halves can follow *quand*. What matters is which action **lasted** and which
**happened**:

*Quand je **suis arrivé**, il **dormait**.* — I arrived (event), he was sleeping (state).
*Quand je **suis arrivé**, il **s'est levé**.* — I arrived, then he got up. Two events, in sequence.
:::

## Signal words

Not rules, but strong hints.

| Points to **imparfait** | Points to **passé composé** |
|---|---|
| toujours, souvent, d'habitude | soudain, tout à coup |
| tous les jours / lundis | un jour, une fois |
| le samedi (every Saturday) | samedi (last Saturday) |
| chaque année | en 2019, hier, ce matin-là |
| pendant que | quand (as a trigger) |
| autrefois, à l'époque | tout de suite, immédiatement |
| en général | deux fois, trois fois |

::: warning A counted repetition is still the passé composé
*Je suis allé en France **trois fois**.* — three times, but countable and finished.
*J'allais en France **tous les ans**.* — habitual, no count.

"Repeated" does not automatically mean imperfect. "Unbounded" does.
:::

## Verbs that change meaning

Some verbs describe a **state** in the imperfect and the **moment it began** in the passé
composé. This is where the distinction stops being stylistic and becomes semantic.

| Verb | Imparfait | Passé composé |
|---|---|---|
| **savoir** | *je savais* — I knew | *j'ai su* — I found out |
| **connaître** | *je connaissais* — I knew (a person) | *j'ai connu* — I met |
| **pouvoir** | *je pouvais* — I was able to | *j'ai pu* — I managed to |
| **ne pas pouvoir** | *je ne pouvais pas* — I couldn't | *je n'ai pas pu* — I failed to |
| **vouloir** | *je voulais* — I wanted | *j'ai voulu* — I decided to / I tried to |
| **devoir** | *je devais* — I was supposed to | *j'ai dû* — I had to / I must have |
| **avoir** | *j'avais peur* — I was afraid | *j'ai eu peur* — I got a fright |
| **être** | *il était malade* — he was ill | *il a été malade* — he fell ill |

::: examples
Je savais qu'il venait. || I knew he was coming.
J'ai su qu'il venait. || I found out he was coming.
Je connaissais Marie. || I knew Marie.
J'ai connu Marie en 2019. || I met Marie in 2019.
Je pouvais le faire. || I was able to do it (capability).
J'ai pu le faire. || I managed to do it (and did).
:::

## Reading a whole paragraph

The mix is easiest to see at length. The imperfect paints; the passé composé acts.

> *Il **était** huit heures et il **pleuvait**. La rue **était** vide. J'**attendais** le bus
> depuis vingt minutes et j'**avais** froid. Soudain, une voiture **s'est arrêtée**. Un homme
> **est descendu** et m'**a demandé** l'heure. Je lui **ai répondu**, et il **est reparti**.*

Everything scene-setting — time, weather, the empty street, waiting, being cold — is imperfect.
Everything that happens — the car stopping, the man getting out, asking, my answering, his
leaving — is passé composé.

::: tip A practical writing habit
Draft your past-tense paragraph in two passes. First write only what **happened**, all in the
passé composé. Then go back and add the **circumstances** around it, all in the imperfect. The
tenses sort themselves out.
:::

## Depuis in the past

*Depuis* forces the imperfect, exactly as it forces the present when talking about now.

::: examples
J'attendais depuis vingt minutes. || I had been waiting for twenty minutes.
Il habitait là depuis 2010. || He had been living there since 2010.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Which tense?
Hier, je ___ (manger) une pizza. || ai mangé — a single event
Quand j'étais petit, je ___ (manger) des pizzas. || mangeais — a habit
Il ___ (pleuvoir) quand je suis sorti. || pleuvait — background
Il ___ (pleuvoir) toute la journée hier. || a plu — a bounded event
Je ___ (lire) quand le téléphone a sonné. || lisais — ongoing
Soudain, la porte ___ (s'ouvrir). || s'est ouverte — *soudain* signals an event
Tous les jours, il ___ (prendre) le bus. || prenait — habit
Je ___ (aller) en France trois fois. || suis allé — counted, bounded
Elle ___ (avoir) vingt ans. || avait — a state
Il ___ (faire) beau ce matin-là. || faisait — description
:::
