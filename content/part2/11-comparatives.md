# Comparatives and superlatives

French comparison is unusually tidy: three words cover every comparison, and the superlative is
the comparative with an article bolted on. The only difficulty is a handful of irregulars.

## The three comparatives

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **plus … que** | more … than | *Il est **plus** grand **que** moi.* |
| **moins … que** | less … than | *Elle est **moins** timide **que** lui.* |
| **aussi … que** | as … as | *Je suis **aussi** fatigué **que** toi.* |

::: examples
Paul est plus grand que Marie. || Paul is taller than Marie.
Ce livre est moins intéressant que l'autre. || …less interesting than…
Elle est aussi intelligente que son frère. || …as intelligent as…
:::

::: rule After que, use a stressed pronoun
*plus grand que **moi***, *que **toi***, *que **lui***, *que **eux*** — never *que je* or
*que il*.
:::

## Comparing adverbs

Same three words, same structure.

::: examples
Il court plus vite que moi. || He runs faster than me.
Elle parle moins fort que lui. || She speaks less loudly than him.
Je travaille aussi souvent que toi. || I work as often as you.
:::

## Comparing quantities: add de

With a **noun**, the comparative needs **de**.

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| **plus de … que** | *J'ai **plus de** livres **que** toi.* |
| **moins de … que** | *Il a **moins d'**argent **que** moi.* |
| **autant de … que** | *Elle a **autant de** travail **que** moi.* |

::: warning aussi for qualities, autant for quantities
*aussi grand que* — as tall as. An adjective.
*autant de livres que* — as many books as. A noun.

Using *aussi de* or *autant grand* is a common slip.
:::

## Comparing verbs

The quantity words stand alone after the verb.

::: examples
Je travaille plus que toi. || I work more than you.
Il mange moins qu'avant. || He eats less than before.
Elle dort autant que moi. || She sleeps as much as I do.
:::

## The irregulars

Four words do not take *plus*.

| Word | Comparative | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **bon** (adj.) | **meilleur** | *plus bon* does not exist |
| **bien** (adv.) | **mieux** | *plus bien* does not exist |
| **mauvais** (adj.) | **pire** or *plus mauvais* | both used |
| **petit** (adj.) | **moindre** or *plus petit* | *moindre* is abstract |

::: rule meilleur vs mieux
This pair defeats a lot of learners. The distinction is the same as *bon* vs *bien*:

- **meilleur** is an **adjective** — it describes a noun. *Ce vin est **meilleur**.*
- **mieux** is an **adverb** — it describes a verb. *Il chante **mieux**.*

*C'est **meilleur*** (it tastes better) vs *c'est **mieux*** (it's better as a situation).
:::

::: examples
Ce vin est meilleur que l'autre. || adjective — describing the wine
Il chante mieux que moi. || adverb — describing how he sings
Je me sens mieux. || I feel better.
C'est la meilleure solution. || the best solution — adjective
C'est mieux comme ça. || it's better this way — adverb
:::

**Meilleur** agrees like any adjective: *meilleur, meilleure, meilleurs, meilleures*. **Mieux**
never changes.

::: warning pire vs plus mauvais
Both are correct. *Pire* is more emphatic and more common in speech: *C'est **pire** que je
pensais.* *Plus mauvais* tends to be used for concrete quality: *un plus mauvais vin*.

The adverb *mal* has *pis* as its comparative, but it survives only in fixed phrases:
***tant pis*** (never mind), *de mal en pis* (from bad to worse).
:::

## Superlatives

> **le / la / les** + **plus** or **moins** + adjective

The article agrees with the noun.

::: examples
le plus grand || the biggest (m.)
la plus grande || the biggest (f.)
les plus grands || the biggest (m. pl.)
la moins chère || the least expensive (f.)
:::

### Position follows the adjective's normal position

If the adjective normally goes **before** the noun, the superlative does too. If it goes
**after**, the article is repeated.

::: examples
le plus grand problème || *grand* goes before — one article
le problème le plus grave || *grave* goes after — article repeated
la plus belle ville || *belle* before
la ville la plus intéressante || *intéressante* after
:::

### de, not "in"

English says *the tallest **in** the class*. French says **de**.

::: examples
le plus grand de la classe || the tallest in the class
la plus belle ville du monde || the most beautiful city in the world
le meilleur restaurant de Paris || the best restaurant in Paris
:::

### Superlative adverbs

Adverbs have no gender, so the article is always **le**.

::: examples
Il court le plus vite. || He runs the fastest.
C'est elle qui travaille le plus. || She's the one who works the most.
Parle le moins fort possible. || Speak as quietly as possible.
:::

### Irregular superlatives

| Base | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| bon | meilleur | **le meilleur** |
| bien | mieux | **le mieux** |
| mauvais | pire | **le pire** |
| petit | moindre | **le moindre** |

::: examples
C'est le meilleur film de l'année. || adjective
C'est ce qu'il fait le mieux. || adverb
C'est le pire. || the worst.
Je n'ai pas la moindre idée. || I haven't the faintest idea.
:::

::: warning The subjunctive after a superlative
A relative clause after a superlative usually takes the subjunctive:
*C'est le meilleur film que j'**aie** jamais vu.* — the best film I've ever seen.

Also after *le seul*, *le premier*, *le dernier*: *C'est la seule chose que je **sache***.
:::

## Expressing progression

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **de plus en plus** | more and more | *Il fait **de plus en plus** froid.* |
| **de moins en moins** | less and less | *J'ai **de moins en moins** de temps.* |
| **plus … plus …** | the more … the more | ***Plus** je travaille, **plus** je suis fatigué.* |
| **moins … moins …** | the less … the less | ***Moins** je dors, **moins** je suis efficace.* |

::: examples
Il fait de plus en plus chaud. || It's getting hotter and hotter.
Plus on est de fous, plus on rit. || The more the merrier.
Moins j'y pense, mieux je me sens. || The less I think about it, the better I feel.
:::
