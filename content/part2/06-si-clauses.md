# Si-clauses

Conditional sentences in French come in exactly **three** patterns. Each pairs a tense in the
*si* half with a tense in the other half, and the pairings are fixed. Learn the three rows below
and conditionals stop being a problem permanently.

## The three patterns

| Type | *si* clause | Main clause | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Real** | **présent** | présent / futur / impératif | it may well happen |
| **2. Unreal present** | **imparfait** | conditionnel présent | it isn't so, but imagine |
| **3. Unreal past** | **plus-que-parfait** | conditionnel passé | it didn't happen, but imagine |

::: rule The si clause never takes a future or a conditional
Whatever else you remember, remember this. After **si** you may write the present, the
imperfect or the pluperfect — never *si je serai*, never *si j'aurais*.

*Si j'aurais su* is the textbook example of bad French. The correct form is *si j'**avais**
su*.
:::

## Type 1: real possibility

> **si** + present → present, future or imperative

The condition is genuinely open. This is the everyday one.

::: examples
Si tu veux, on peut partir. || If you want, we can leave.
S'il pleut, je resterai à la maison. || If it rains, I'll stay home.
Si tu as le temps, appelle-moi. || If you have time, call me.
Si tu chauffes l'eau à 100 degrés, elle bout. || A general truth.
:::

::: note si becomes s' only before il and ils
*S'il pleut*, *s'ils viennent* — but *si elle vient*, *si on part*, *si Anne arrive*. The
elision is narrower than for other words.
:::

## Type 2: unreal present or future

> **si** + imperfect → present conditional

The condition is contrary to fact, or unlikely. The imperfect here has nothing to do with the
past — it is simply the form this pattern requires.

::: examples
Si j'avais de l'argent, j'achèterais une maison. || If I had money, I'd buy a house. (I don't.)
Si j'étais toi, je ne dirais rien. || If I were you, I'd say nothing.
Si tu venais, on serait content. || If you came, we'd be pleased.
Si on gagnait au loto, on voyagerait. || If we won the lottery, we'd travel.
:::

::: warning The English "if I were" trap
English marks this with a special subjunctive: *if I **were** you*. French does not — it just
uses the ordinary imperfect: *si j'**étais** toi*. There is no French subjunctive after *si*,
ever.
:::

## Type 3: unreal past

> **si** + pluperfect → past conditional

The condition did not happen, and it is too late. This is the tense of regret.

::: examples
Si j'avais su, je serais venu. || If I had known, I'd have come.
Si tu m'avais appelé, j'aurais répondu. || If you had called, I'd have answered.
S'il avait plu, on serait restés. || If it had rained, we'd have stayed.
Si elle était partie plus tôt, elle n'aurait pas raté le train. || …she wouldn't have missed the train.
:::

## Either half can come first

The order is free; only a comma changes.

::: examples
Si j'avais su, je serais venu. || si clause first — comma required
Je serais venu si j'avais su. || main clause first — no comma
:::

## Mixing types 2 and 3

A past condition can have a present consequence, and vice versa. The two halves keep their own
logic.

::: examples
Si j'avais étudié, je parlerais français maintenant. || Past condition, present result.
Si j'étais plus organisé, j'aurais fini hier. || Present condition, past result.
:::

## Other uses of si

**si** does not always introduce a condition.

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| indirect question — *whether* | *Je ne sais pas **si** il vient.* — I don't know whether he's coming. |
| suggestion, with the imperfect | ***Si** on allait au cinéma ?* — How about the cinema? |
| *yes*, contradicting a negative | *— Tu ne viens pas ? — **Si** !* |
| intensifier — *so* | *Il est **si** gentil.* |

::: warning si = whether allows the future
The ban on the future after *si* applies only to **conditional** *si*. When *si* means
*whether*, the future is fine:

*Je ne sais pas s'il **viendra**.* — I don't know whether he will come. Correct.
*S'il **viendra**, je serai content.* — Wrong; this is conditional *si*.

If you can substitute *whether* in English, the future is allowed.
:::

## Alternatives to si

| Expression | Followed by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **au cas où** | conditional | *Au cas où il **pleuvrait**, prends un parapluie.* |
| **à condition que** | subjunctive | *À condition qu'il **vienne**.* |
| **à moins que** | subjunctive | *À moins qu'il ne **pleuve**.* |
| **sinon** | — | *Dépêche-toi, **sinon** on va être en retard.* |
| **même si** | indicative | *Même s'il pleut, on sort.* |

::: warning au cas où takes the conditional
It is the one *if*-like expression that does: *au cas où tu **aurais** besoin de moi*. Not the
subjunctive, not the future.
:::

## Practice

::: exercise Complete the pattern
Si tu ___ (vouloir), on peut partir. || veux — type 1
S'il ___ (pleuvoir), je resterai. || pleut — type 1
Si j'___ (avoir) de l'argent, j'achèterais une maison. || avais — type 2
Si j'étais toi, je ___ (ne pas dire) ça. || ne dirais pas — type 2
Si j'___ (savoir), je serais venu. || avais su — type 3
Si tu m'avais appelé, j'___ (répondre). || aurais répondu — type 3
:::

::: exercise Right or wrong?
*Si j'aurais su…* || Wrong. *Si j'avais su…* — never a conditional after conditional *si*.
*Si je serai riche…* || Wrong. *Si je suis riche…* — never a future either.
*Je ne sais pas s'il viendra.* || Correct. Here *si* means *whether*, so the future is allowed.
*Si j'étais toi…* || Correct. The imperfect, not a subjunctive.
*Au cas où il pleuvrait…* || Correct. *Au cas où* takes the conditional.
:::

::: exercise Which type is it?
*Si tu as le temps, appelle-moi.* || Type 1 — a real possibility.
*Si on gagnait au loto, on voyagerait.* || Type 2 — unreal present.
*S'il avait plu, on serait restés.* || Type 3 — unreal past.
*Si j'avais étudié, je parlerais français.* || Mixed — past condition, present result.
:::

If it rains, I'll stay home. || S'il pleut, je resterai à la maison.

:::
